31. The Three Taverns Inn

AD59 Three Taverns Inn, near Rome

26 years after the crucifixion

``Hear the new agreement which God in his mercy has given us and nothing can daunt us. We aren't in the business of using no hocus-pocus or clever tricks; we don't manipulate the Word of God for our human benefit. All we do is speak the plain truth. It's been suggested that our Gospel is `veiled', that it is somehow hidden and secret. I don't see how. If there is such a `veil', then it must be in the minds of those who aren't listening. Perhaps the `spirit' of this world has blindfolded the minds of those who do not believe, and thus prevents the broad day light of the glorious Gospel of Christ, the image of God, from shining on them.

``What do we preach? Do we glorify ourselves? Far from it. What we preach is Christ Jesus the Lord. I his service, I am your servant for his sake. The same God, who first ordered 'light to shine in darkness', has latterly flooded our hearts with his light, and we share that light to enlighten men only by sharing the knowledge of the glory of God.

``We don't hold up a fancy precious container and say `this holds the source of all goodness', but rather we keep this priceless treasure in an everyday earthenware jar. Why? Because the splendid power of it belongs to God and not to us.

``We may be handicapped in our work on all sides, but we are never frustrated; we are puzzled at the resistance of this world, but we never despair; we have been persecuted, but we never have to endure that alone; we may be knocked down from time to time, but we cannot be never knocked out! Each and every day we experience some part of the death of the Lord Jesus, but that only means that we also come to know the power of the life of Jesus in these bodies of ours. Yes, we who are living are always being exposed to death for Jesus' sake, so that the life of Jesus may be plainly seen in our mortal lives. We are always facing death, but this means that you know more and more of life. Our faith is like that mentioned in the scripture: `I believed and therefore I spoke'.

``It's true: we believe, and because we believe and we see the truth that it is there for the taking by all men, we cannot but speak out and share the truths that we have received. And remember this: we know for certain that he who raised the Lord Jesus from death shall also by him raise us.

``And we shall all stand together in that day with Jesus before God.

``The more you see how this is working out for your benefit - how the more grace God gives, the more thanksgiving will redound to his glory - the more strength you will have in the Lord. This is the reason why we never collapse. It does seem that the `outward' man suffers wear and tear, but at the same time the `inward' man receives fresh strength.

``These little troubles which, lets face it, are really so transitory are the means by which we become able to win a much more permanent, glorious and genuine reward - one that is out of all proportion to our pain. Why? Because instead of paying attention to all those little niggling things that continually press us for our acknowledgment, we concentrate on the so-called `invisible' things that come from our understanding of the Lord.

``And the truth, my friends? It is these so-called `invisible' things which are actually real and eternal, not the gross things of our everyday existence.

``Consider if you will, the transition we know as death. Imagine taking down a tent in the morning after you have slept in it. Is that the end of the sleeper? Hardly! We still have a permanent home that we will return to. It is the same with the body! If the body is `taken down', we know, that we can return to our permanent house in Heaven; not a mortal body made by mortal means, but a permanent body made by God.

``Right now, we feel that deep longing for our heavenly home, but at the same time, we don't want our current home to `collapse' because we would feel unprotected against the elements. And we have that fear of change: it's not that we don't want to be at one with God, but we fear to lose the body that we know on a daily basis and fear being left homeless.

``And God has planned ahead and knows what we want and has a plan to enable us to get there. And because he sees the fear that comes from our uncertainty, he has given us his Spirit as a guarantee of its truth. This makes us confident, whatever happens. We realise that while we continue to see ourselves as living in the body we cannot be fully present with the Lord, and so if we want to be fully with him, we must trust in him without seeing him. We are so sure of this that we would really rather leave the body through death and thus come to be living with the Lord.

``Since it is our aim to please God, whether we are `at home' or `in our tent'. Remember that each and every one of us will on that day have to stand without pretence before Christ our judge, and we shall be rewarded for what we did when we lived in our bodies, whether it was good or bad.

``What we are is utterly plain to God - just as what each of us is individually is clear to that individual throught his conscience. The very spring of our actions is the love of Christ. We look at it like this: if one died for all men then, in a sense, they all died, and his purpose in dying for them is that their lives should now be no longer lived for themselves but for him who died and rose again for them.

``All of this means that while we think we know men based on their outward lives, this isn't really valid. When a man is baptised into Christ he becomes a new person altogether - he is `born again'; born of the spirit and not of the flesh - and his past is finished and gone; everything becomes fresh and new. All this is God's doing, for he has reconciled us to himself through Jesus Christ; and he has made us agents of the reconciliation. God was in Christ personally reconciling the world to himself - not counting their sins against them - and has commissioned us with the message of reconciliation. We are now Christ's ambassadors, as though God were appealing direct to you through us. As his personal representatives we say, `Make your peace with God.' For God caused Christ, who himself knew nothing of sin, actually to be sin for our sakes, so that in Christ we might be made good with the goodness of God.

``As co-operators with God himself we beg you then, not to fail to accept the grace of God. For God's word is - `In an acceptable time I have heard you, and in the day of salvation I have helped you'. And when is the `acceptable time'? I'll tell you: it's right now. This very day is the `day of salvation' that God was talking about.

``As far as we are concerned we do not wish to stand in anyone's way, nor do we wish to bring discredit on the ministry God has given us. Indeed we want to prove ourselves genuine ministers of God whatever we have to go through - patient endurance of troubles or even disasters, being flogged or imprisoned; being mobbed, having to work like slaves, having to go without food or sleep. All this we want to meet with sincerity, with insight and patience; by sheer kindness and the Holy Spirit; with genuine love, speaking the plain truth, and living by the power of God. Our sole defence, our only weapon, is a life of integrity, whether we meet honour or dishonour, praise or blame. Even when we are called `impostors' we must be honest and forthright; when called `nobodies' we must be in the public eye. We are never far from death, but while we are here, we are alive, always `going through it' yet never being `milled under'. We know sorrow in our daily lives, yet our inner joy is inextinguishable. We have `nothing to bless ourselves with' yet we bless many others with true riches. We are penniless, but we have everything worth having.

``We are hiding nothing from you and our hearts are absolutely open to you. Don't link up with unbelievers and try to work with them. What common interest can there be between goodness and evil? How can light and darkness share life together? How can there be harmony between Christ and the devil? What business can a believer have with an unbeliever? What common ground can idols hold with the temple of God? For we, remember, are ourselves living temples of the living God, as God has said: 'I will dwell in them and walk among them. I will be their God, and they shall be my people'.

``Wherefore: `Come out from among them and be separate, says the Lord. Do not touch what is unclean, and I will receive you'. `I will be a father to you, and you shall be my sons and daughters, says the Lord Almighty'.

``With these promises ringing in our ears, dear friends, let us keep clear of anything that smirches body or soul. Let us prove reverence for God by consecrating ourselves to him completely.

``My friends, let us live our lives with that certainty of faith which enables us to overcome everything. I may be a prisoner; I may yet be condemned for telling the truth; but I have no fear because of the promise that is in Christ Jesus.''
