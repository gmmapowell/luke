26. Nicodemus

AD57 Jerusalem

24 years after the crucifixion

I met Nicodemus at the Temple.

Not in the inner courts of course; I was not allowed there. We met in the Court of the Gentiles, near the Great Gate where Paul had been arrested the previous year.

Nicodemus was now retired, but had been one of the senior members of the Sanhedrin and an early critic of Jesus in his ministry in Galilee. He had gone there to try and determine whether Jesus was indeed a prophet or just a rabble-rousing revolutionary.

He had come away changed.

``I thought I was going to meet a fraud,'' he told me that sunny afternoon before I was due to head back to Caesarea.

``I'd heard such stories about him - the clarity of his teaching and judgments; the healings; the huge crowds that followed him - that I figured it had to be made up by his surrogates to make him seem important. Surely nobody could be that perfect.

``Nobody apart from the Messiah, of course, but I don't think - even if I'd heard it suggested that he could be the Messiah - that I'd considered that possibility seriously. He might be a teacher; he might be a zealot; he might even just be a lunatic who was convincing enough to fool the simple people of Galilee.

``I had a friend in Capernaum, so I went and visited him, and while I was there I made contact with Jesus through some of his associates known to my friend and invited him to join us for dinner. He agreed readily enough, which surprised me at the time, but I later learnt that he had a number of influential backers and possibly thought that I might want to join them. And a free meal is a free meal.

``When he arrived, I greeted him. Not, it has to be said, as a superior, but as what he was: an itinerant preacher. And we sat down to eat.

``At first, the conversation was of no consequence. We discussed the usual topics: the weather, the prospects for the harvest, and the Roman occupation. None of these things are causes for dissent among the people.

``Even so, all his answers were both guarded and cryptic. It was not like a normal conversation with ordinary folk.

``But then the conversation turned to his mission and his preaching. And I asked him about the life that he was said to live, associating with prostitutes, drunkards and publicani.

`` `What of it?' he responded. `Consider John, if you will. He wanders the wilderness, surviving on water from the Jordan, locust beans and anything else he can find. And what do men say of him? That he is mad. He may be holy, but he is insane.

`` `And I come along, wandering among the towns where people are, preaching to the lost sheep of Israel - the poor, the hungry, the disposessed - and to those who avoid those fates by doing what they have to to survive - collect taxes or sell their bodies - and they criticize me saying ``he is himself a drunkard, a glutton and an adulterer.''

`` `Tell me this, how then should I behave? To whom should I preach the word of repentance? To the rich and holy? Would they listen? Do they feel the need of repentance?'

``I had nothing really to say to that, so I said nothing.

`` `Indeed, consider yourself. Would you listen to my preaching? Do you feel the need to repent?'

``I had to admit that I did not. I knew of nothing for which I had need of repentance.

``We looked at each other for a few moments. And then the most remarkable thing happened. Somehow, a woman had managed to barge her way past the door guards and into the dining room we were using. I didn't know her, as such, but I knew her type: she was obviously a prostitute.

``And she had brought with her the tools of her trade: a large box of perfume was in her hands. And she knelt down behind Jesus, and burst into tears. And she used that to wash his feet, and then dried them with her hair. And then she went on to put the unclean perfume on his feet.

``And all the while, Jesus just stared at me, defying me to say something. And I looked back at him, knowing what kind of woman this was. And I knew he knew, but he did not care. It made no sense.

``And then she stopped, and just placed her head on the floor and carried on crying. Not wailing like they do at funerals, but just a gentle sobbing as if she had suffered a great loss or hurt.

``And Jesus turned to me and asked me what seemed a very simple question.

`` `Imagine if you will, a creditor with two debtors. One owes a fortune, while the other a debt so small he hardly notices it. One day the creditor tells both of them that since it is the year of Jubilees, they are both forgiven. How will they react?'

``I continued to look at him for a few more moments before answering.

`` `I am sure they will both be glad,' I replied. `But the one who is forgiven a fortune will be ecstatic and fall down on his knees, while the other will probably just shake his hand and thank him.'

`` `Indeed so,' countered Jesus. `You do actually see this woman, I take it? You think of her sins, and to you they seem many, an unbearable burden. And so it is. No amount of preaching of the Law will lift those sins from her, with her life as it is.

`` `But the grace of God can wash away sins, even as John does by the Jordan. Her sins, many as they are, have been forgiven.' And he turned and gently pulled the woman up from the floor. `My dear,' he said, `all your sins are forgiven by the Lord; you have been washed clean and given a fresh start. Go in peace, and sin no more.'

``I couldn't believe what I had seen. It seemed to me that Jesus literally viewed this woman as being better than me. A woman who was a prostitute of more value that a Temple Priest? He must be out of his mind.

``I've heard many people who have talked about their conversions say it all happened in a moment. Maybe it did for me, but I certainly didn't realize it at the time. Rather, it dawned on me slowly how insular I had been; how hierarchical, viewing the kingdom of God as surely being organized much as the kingdoms of this world.

``But in time my eyes were opened and I saw Jesus as he really was: God's Messiah.''
