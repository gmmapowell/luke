1. Paul

AD 50 Troas

17 years since the crucifixion of Jesus

I sat in shock.

Lydia, my mistress, had sent me along on this trip to Troas for a number of reasons including my desire to consult with some of the medical experts over here in Asia. But without question, her most important assignment for me was to track down Saul of Tarsus, commonly called after his Roman name Paullus - or Paul - and hear what he had to say.

She had first heard of him many years ago from travellers coming from Antioch. We had heard of him numerous times since then as he travelled through the south-west of Asia. But only recently had we heard that he had come into the province.

Lydia was one of the 'god-fearers'. The Jewish religion was very exclusive; you didn't have to be born into it, but it certainly helped. Apart from anything else, they had any number of bizarre and unusual practices and restrictions all of which came under the general heading of "the Law of Moses" or, among themselves, simply "the Law".

"The Law" was the great stumbling-block to outsiders - or Gentiles as they were referred to by the Jews - becoming fully-fledged "adopted" Jews, or proselytes. The biggest single obstacle was the requirement for males to undergo circumcision, but even for females such as my mistress, the various complex dietary requirements made daily life outside of a specifically Jewish community almost impossible. And a trade such as purple manufacture - the business in which Lydia was occupied - with its smelly chemicals and noisome environment left one in a state of almost permanent "uncleanness" under the Law.

But there was no doubt that the Jews were sober, dependable and virtuous. In a decadent age such as this, that is unquestionably a good thing. Many wished to be closer to that, to hear how to live more like that, to be encouraged to live more like that; in short, how to be like that while at the same time not being subject to the apparently arbitrary other restrictions in the Law.

Paul, we had heard cryptically, preached an alternative to the Law. Exactly what it was had not been made clear by any of our interlocutors. They merely repeated that everywhere he went, he preached that God had come in person and had freed us from the Law. Somehow. But did that freedom just lead us back to the iniquity of the Romans? Whatever it was he was specifically preaching, we heard that in every community he visited, a division rapidly emerged between the Jews and the full Proselytes on the one side, and the 'god-fearers' like ourselves on the other. The Jews, it would seem, objected vociferously to the idea that their cherished Law - that they literally believed to have been given to their leader Moses while he stood on top of a mountain somewhere near Egypt - might be questioned, let alone be made obsolete. They saw themselves as "special", as "the chosen people" of their God, who, by rigid adherence to their code of laws, would be given the first place in line at the end of days when, they believed, rewards would be handed out by their God - or possibly one of his `agents'.

Some, we had heard, believed that this Paul either believed himself to be one of these `agents' or to be acting on behalf of one of them. But none of the stories had been clear. Nothing had really made sense. Which is why, hearing that he was just across the Aegean sea, Lydia had despatched me to hear him, talk to him and hopefully bring him over to Philippi.

I had come. I had heard. And now it all made sense.

Everything that everybody had said at one time or another clicked into place for me as he spoke. All those cryptic comments were a part of the truth, much as when you see a strange animal - such as Elephant - for the first time after hearing it described so many times. What Paul said was all of the things I'd heard - and yet was not constrained by them.

We were in one of the Jewish meetng halls scattered around the empire - synagogues they are called. To be clear, they are not to be confused with Temples - the Jews only have one Temple, and that is in Jerusalem and all their `true' religious acts take place there. The synagogues are places of instruction and teaching. The meetings proceed by having readings from their holy writings - the aforementioned Law of Moses, along with the various collected sayings of their prophets, and then having one or more of the congregation stand up and expound on particular points.

Paul had done more than expound on the readings. I understood why devout Jews could object so violently to his teachings.

And yet, and yet ... was this not exactly what my mistress Lydia wanted to hear? That the promise of a good life rewarded was available to all? That the contents of the heart mattered more that an adherence to an obscure law code?

To look at, Paul was not an impressive man. Short, pale and thin, in ordinary life he had a somewhat careworn and diffident appearance. A nervous, uncertain man who looked harried and tossed on the storms of daily life. But when he stood up to preach "his Gospel" - his account of the life, death and re-birth of the man he called Jesus - he was transformed; as if, in the act of standing, he was possessed by another. He literally seemed to grow taller, more imposing. His otherwise unsteady, reedy voice calmed down and boomed. He became, in short, the prophet that he seemed to claim to be.

After the meeting was over, many flocked around him, wanting to know more. Some, taking issue with his teaching, disputed whether he had the authority to preach as he did. Others wanted to know more and to join with this "Way" of which he spoke. Others just stood around listening to the discussion, trying to understand if this was truly a "new thing" - or if he was just a madman full of his own self-importance.

In consequence, it was more than an hour before the room had cleared out enough that it was worth my while approaching the small knot of men left in the room in the fading light from the almost-dead fire. The meeting had started late - the traditional meeting time being shortly after dark on a Friday - and just gone later and later. First, from the long and impassioned speech that Paul had given, and during which he was frequently interrupted and heckled. Then from the crowds mobbing him after the event. It was now past midnight.

There were probably only about five men left in the synagogue when I approached close enough for Paul to see me properly in the faded light. When he did, I was astounded by his reaction.

``You!'' he screeched. ``It is you who has been haunting my dreams! Tell me, tell me, are you not? You are from Macedonia, correct? And you implore me to come there because of your need for the gospel?''

I was struck dumb. How did he know? What did he mean about haunting his dreams? Had we met before somewhere and I had not known? His nervous demeanour had vanished again and he was staring at me intently; his eyes on fire with passion.

He reached out and grasped my arm, pulling me towards him for a closer look. ``Yes'', he confirmed to himself. ``You are the one, a slave from Macedonia. Tell me, what is it that you want?''

``My mistress,'' I stammered, finally recovering some limited power of speech and still in a state of shock, both from his earlier preaching and his apparent recognition of me.

I tried again.

``My mistress is among the God-fearers at Philippi. She wishes to be one of the elect, but cannot give up her business dying purple. She hears ... she has heard of you and that you preach a different deliverance - of the heart and not of the body. She wants ... she wants to know if you could bring this deliverance to Philippi. If you could `convert' her in a way that the Jews cannot.''

``I, myself, am a Jew,'' retorted Paul. ``Nothing can I do that cannot be done by a Jew. Jesus, also, was a Jew. A Jew born under the Law, subject to the Law, and who died according to the Law.

``But through his death and resurrection he showed the limits of the Law and the true Grace of the Holy One which transcends the Law. That Grace extends to all, regardless of nation, race, gender or status. She is free to accept the Grace of God.''

``But how?'' I mustered. ``I hear your words, but in Philippi we do not have any who can show us this way. We have heard of you from travellers, but the messages they bring are so garbled it is hard to make out what they might mean.''

``I am headed to Bithynia,'' Paul said. ``I must spread the word to all of Asia.''

``And what about Europe?'' I asked. ``Are we not worthy to hear the revealed word of God? We need your help.''

Paul stared at me as if struck.

``The very words!'' he exclaimed. ``The very words of the man in the dream. Tell me you are not him!''

How could I tell him who he had or had not seen in his dream?

After the others had left, we walked back to the lodgings my mistress had arranged for us.

Troas is built on the plain in front of the legendary old city of Troy. They say that Troy itself was built into the mountainside behind the city of Troas. I don't know about that, but there is a hill in front of the mountains that they say is the burial mound of Old Troy.

The modern town is a harbour town, built on the banks of the Maeander and flourishes on the trade through the Bosphorus, linking Cimmeria and Pontus with Bithynia and Greece. When the winds are northerly, whole fleets will end up watering here until the winds change direction, much as they say Agamemnon was forced to wait in Greece to cross the Aegean all those years ago.

So to say that we were staying `near the port' wouldn't really be very descriptive - not much of Troas was not near the port. But, understandably, captains and other with rank and wealth enough to opt not to stay on their ships while they are in port wanted to stay relatively close to them, and thus most of the lodgings for transients were within a hundred yards of the docks lining the Maeander. And that was where we stayed.

Six of us had come altogether, servants of my mistress, to negotiate for raw materials and other products for the dyeing business. That didn't include a ship and its crew; when the deals were done, we would arrange for carriage on suitable ships at an appropriate time.

Although it was early in the morning by the time we turned up at the hostel, nobody batted an eyelid. This was a port town and people came and went at all times. Although not Alexandria to have a giant lighthouse burning through the night, signal fires where generally kept burning on either side of the river to enable those trying to navigate the channel at night to do so safely.

Ganymede opened his eyes and looked at us as we came in, then rolled over in his bunk and went back to sleep. Life as a slave is like that: there are real risks, but once you've identified that something is not a threat, sleep is usually the better option. Whatever Paul might look like, he did not look threatening.

We sat down opposite on my bunk and continued talking in low tones so as not to disturb the others in the room more than necessary.

``How do you get by?'' I asked.

``I work, doing whatever comes to hand,'' he replied. ``Usually casual and manual things come easiest. Wherever there's a substantial army encampment, I'll generally work building tents or shelters for the army. They always need work and often find it hard to get people who want to work for them.''

''You work for the Romans?'' I exclaimed, louder that I really had intended to.

``What do the Romans matter?'' he replied, apparently in complete sincerity. ``Within a few years, all that we've known will be swept away and replaced with by the coming Kingdom of God. Claudius will be gone; Messalina will be gone; the wicked and corrupt governors will be gone. How could I delay this important work for a meaningless scruple about working for a doomed, idolatrous empire?''

When he put it like that, I didn't really see any point arguing. The way he saw the world was not just different, it was ... radical. Anything and everything came second to preaching the word of God to anyone who would listen - and often to those who had no interest in listening either.

``We've heard you've run into trouble a number of times,'' I offered, thinking that the tales we had been told were likely exaggerated.

``What of it? Yes, I've been beaten, stoned, thrown into jail. A few months back outside Derbe, I was left for dead after a beating and a stoning for `saying the wrong things'. But they just don't understand ... the Spirit burning within me is so much stronger than anything they can do. Even were they to kill me, I don't believe it would be the end any more than it was for Jesus.''

``You need to come with us,'' I blurted, unable to contain it any longer. ``We in Europe need you; need your words, your message, your conviction. I assure you that you will receive all the support you need from my mistress.''

``Well then,'' said Paul. ``When do we leave?''

``The others still have their business to complete. But by the middle of next week we should be ready to go.''

``That works for me,'' he replied. ``Can I sleep here? I don't have a place of my own.''

I looked at him blankly, and then stood up off `my' bed and let him have it. He probably needed sleep more than I did.

I needed to think.
