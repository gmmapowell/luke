21. Jerusalem

AD57 Jerusalem

24 years after the crucifixion

It took me a while to make it there.

Paul continued to remain in custody, relatively pleasant though it was being under house arrest in Philip's house, working and preaching with the others.

But as one year rolled into the next I found the time to take the journey down to Jerusalem, remembering as I went the same journey Paul and I had taken the previous year - before he had been arrested.

Philip had provided me with basic background information about a number of people with whom he felt I should talk, and whose stories would be interesting to hear.

Top of this list - and apparently the most interesting of them all - was Mary from the Galilean hill town of Magdala. According to Philip, she had had a colourful reputation there before meeting Jesus, at which point her life changed direction.

It's a two day journey each way from Caesarea to Jerusalem, and I couldn't afford to be away too long, so I had about six days in Jerusalem to track down and talk to all the people I wanted to see. Fortunately, the year before I had learned some of the geography of the place and met a number of people that would be useful to me.

As I approached from the north, the mountain - Mount Zion - on which the city stands reared up before me. As a year before, I began the arduous climb up towards the summit.

Mary lived in a house on a narrow street not far from the temple with a number of other members of the Way. As with all members, work, money, food and preaching all overlapped each other. The house held six members and Mary was not at home when I arrived. I was shown up onto the roof terrace where there was a shaded sitting area and drank water with my hosts until she returned home.

I was immediately struck with both her grace and humility. This woman was one of the leaders of the Way in Jerusalem, but unlike Peter or Philip did not seem burdened by the responsibility of that role. It sat lightly on her, and when she smiled at me I felt the grace of God descend on me. I rose to greet her.

``Mary, your humble servant is Luke, associate of the evangelist Paul.''

``Why so formal, good man? Of course I know of you; I saw you last year with Peter and Paul at the Temple.''

But I had not noticed here; strange, since her presence now seemed hard to ignore.

``How can I be of assistance? I am told you come to me on Philip's recommendation?''

How to explain? I didn't really know myself, except that Philip's story had made me interested in knowing more about more of the people who had known Jesus - and maybe even to come closer to knowing Jesus himself. I did my best to put it into words.

``How delightful! You want to know how I converted from the heathen temptress to a servant of God! Well, it is a simple tale, and I'm sorry to say you'll be distressed at how boring it is by the time I've finished.

``But yes, I knew Jesus. As well as anyone else, I suppose - for much of his ministry I was with him, along with `the twelve'. A number of others accompanied him as well - you should probably talk to Susanna; Joanna has died now, of course.

``Unlike John, Jesus was never a `wild man'. He was, you might say, boringly normal, except in one regard: you couldn't even hope to keep a secret from him. Let's face it: in his presence, you couldn't even hope to keep a secret from yourself.

``He had these penetrating eyes. He would look at you and ... it would seem like he looked right through you. And when I met him, well, I'm sure you've heard that when I met him I was supporting myself the old-fashioned way, taking money for being with men. And I'd heard that this man was in the neighbourhood, so I thought I'd go and see what he was up to. I mean, holy men are generally like others, they have needs ...

``I was living in Magdala at the time. I grew up there, although I think I was born a little further north. But basically I lived there up until I met Jesus; then I followed him until he was crucified and then I settled here.

``But I'm getting ahead of myself. I had heard he was preaching nearby, so I went to hear him. And I was amazed. The things he had to say - well, they weren't the normal drivel you hear from these men. He cared. I don't know about you, but generally when you hear holy men, their first interest is in getting you to believe in whatever they have going on and only secondarily do their care about what you have going on.

``But Jesus was different. He would preach and tell stories, yes, but he would walk through the crowds that followed him and meet with each of them as individuals and greet them and talk to them and address their needs. When people came needing healing, he would heal them; when they came with problems, he would address them. When people asked for help, he would give it.

``But never for free. The price of meeting Jesus was change. And for some the price of change was too high and they would walk away, refusing the gifts he brought because of the price he charged. And such a one was I.

``I heard Jesus preach and I dismissed his words as words. They sounded fair and, as I say, he clearly cared. And then he came up to me and said, and I remember this to this day, `Much sin weighs you down, young lady, much sin. But, remember, you can at once put down that load and start again. Remember that any woman who wishes can be born again; born not of flesh seeking its end in flesh, but born of the spirit, seeking the Spirit!'

``As I'm sure you can imagine, nobody wants to hear that from a preacher. I felt unclean, judged, found wanting. And I stormed off home, and my `mother' asked me what was wrong, and I told her what had happened. She shrugged it off: there would always be do-gooders and I should just ignore them.

``And I did my best to do just that. And I moved on with my life and making my living as best I could. I was young then, and there was no shortage of men who would pay handsomely to be with me.

``And then one day I heard that Jesus was visiting with one of the esteemed Rabbis in Magdala. And I burst into tears.

``At that very moment it struck me: the thing that stood between me and what I actually wanted to be was my pride. My pride that he had humbled by showing me who I really was. And I gathered up my cloak and a jar of perfumed oil that I used to prepare myself for clients and went straight over to the Rabbi's house.

``How I made it past the doorman I don't now recall. Probably he had been a client at some point and thought that I would repay him for his generosity.

``Jesus was there at the table and I knew what I had to do; what I should have done when I first met him. I washed his feet and dried them with my hair; I took the perfume and applied it, right there at the table with the Rabbi looking on, horrified - he knew who I was, of course, and would have no dealings with me.

``Jesus looked at his expression and actually laughed. What a wonderful sound! Not a harsh, mocking laugh, but one that saw through everything in the situation and took us all with him.

`` `It's all very well to laugh, Jesus,' said the Rabbi. `But do you know who this is that is perfuming your body? And how she pays for that perfume?'

`` `But of course, I do,' he replied. `I met her the other day in the fields. You, I noticed, were not there with your flock. And I spoke to her and told her what she needed to do in order to obtain forgiveness of her sins, many though they may be.

`` `And at the time, she balked at my offer. Sins may be heavy to carry, but to lay them down can sometimes be even harder. But you see her now, sealing her bargain of repentance. You ask after the perfume; could she give any stronger token of her change of heart than that the essential tool of her trade - that which makes her desirable to others - she is giving away to a man who has no need of it?

`` `Consider this, if you will. My task here is not to congratulate those fortunate enough to be able to live lives of holiness, but rather to recall those who have sinned. It is much like a moneylender who has clients who are in debt and he knows cannot of their own volition repay the debts.

`` `If he has two clients, one of whom is just lightly in debt, while the other is heavily in debt, and he says to both of them that they are forgiven, how will they react?

`` `The one who is deeply in debt will shout for joy and fall at his feet and kiss him and promise him anything within his power; the other will be grateful to be sure, but will still have a secret thought that maybe, given a little more time, he could have repaid this debt.

`` `Such ingratitude can be a sore that never needs to be lanced but nevertheless continues to abrade.

`` `This woman has seen the weight of her sin, and has chosen to unload it. Her repentenance is forever and she has seen the Way of Truth.

`` `Have you?'

``The Rabbi was shocked. To see himself placed below me on a scale of holiness was beyond his ability to comprehend. But by the end of that evening, two souls had been saved and written into the book of life.

``If you want to meet those whom Jesus saved, you should certainly talk to Nicodemus.''

Certainly I should, but I was here to talk to Mary, and she was, allegedly, the person who had first seen Jesus alive and well after he had been first crucified and then resurrected - to Paul, the central act of his life.

``To Paul, it probably would be. To me, the central act of his life was when he looked at me, a woman beneath anyone's notice. And freely forgave me of all my sins. Compared to that, raising himself from the dead hardly seems to signify. I can see why others, who never had a personal experience of Jesus would see it differently.

``But yes, I was there that morning. I woke up early - truth be told, I'd really not slept either night after Mary (his mother) and I, along with Joseph, Nicodemus and the rest had placed his body in Joseph's grave. Anyway, when it started to hint at becoming light - the moment, in short, that it was acceptable to move - I went out and set off for the tomb.

``When I arrived, the sky was glowing but it wasn't quite dawn yet. Everything was misty and washed-out. I knew the way - I hadn't thought of really anything else for the past day and a half - and I walked there almost in a trance.

``All of which meant that when I arrived, I just couldn't immediately place what I was seeing. Some have said since then that I didn't see what I've said I saw - but I certainly know what I experienced.

``The last thing that we had done was to roll the stone across the entrance to the tomb. I wasn't so foolish as to think that I could move it by myself - it had taken all our combined strength to put it in place - but I was ready to wait until the others arrived. Since I hadn't been able to sleep, I knew I was early and expected to have to wait until the others arise. But when I was close enough, I realized that the stone had already been removed.

``I couldn't immediately imagine how that had been done - but it occurred to me that Joseph could have brought some help and they could have arrived before me. So I walked into the tomb to start working on the enbalming.

``But there was no body there!''

As she said it, I could see the expression on her face reflecting the inner turmoil she had felt as she relived the experience once again. For all Paul's preaching of the risen Christ - passionate as it seems when he's delivering it - it pales compared to the intense emotion this woman must have felt in the moment of the act's accomplishment. I was almost transported with her back to the site of the tomb. I made a mental note to visit the tomb myself - not that I expected to have the same emotionally transforming experience.

``I looked around, but I couldn't see Joseph either. Or anyone else. But the temporary linen graveclothes that we had wrapped Jesus in were lying there on the plinth. It made no sense.

``I stepped back outside and into the slowly dawning light. As I did, I saw Joanna arriving but I had no words. I just gestured into the tomb. She went in and came out again with the same look of shock on her face that I must have had. We hadn't even begun to figure out what to say to each other when the angels appeared and said that Jesus was risen and reminded us of the prophecies - some that he had made himself - that his journey to Jerusalem would end in death, but at the same time not end until all the world had heard his name.

``I can't claim that I understood everything in that moment, but I understood one thing very clearly: this story was not over in the way that I had been thinking it had been since he was crucified. In some sense - a sense not clear to me yet - he was still alive and at large in the world.

``I didn't know what to do, but fortunately Joanna had a clearer head. She said that while the apostles were still in hiding, she knew where Peter was at least - because he had spent the Sabbath in her house. We decided to go back there and seek his advice. You have to understand that at the time it was not clear what was going on - it wasn't the way it sounds when you know the whole story.

``Well, we went back to Joanna's house. I couldn't even begin to catalogue all the emotions that were running through me. On the one hand, I was elated - I couldn't really process what the angels had told me, but I was excited that the story might not be over; on the other hand, Jesus was gone and I had no idea where; then again, I had no idea what was going to happen next or how to make anybody believe me.

``When we arrived at Joanna's, it was fully dawn. We weren't the only ones who knew that Peter was there, and most of the disciples had gathered by that time. We made our report, and most of them just dismissed it as foolishness. But Peter seemed somehow struck dumb by what we had said, and then, in the middle of the hubbub and commotion, seemed to come to life.

`` `He is risen you say? In accordance with the prophecy? The tomb is open?'

``I nodded agreement to all these questions. And then before I could blink he was out of the door and running to the tomb to see for himself.

``He was much more sober when he returned. He walked in slowly, deep in thought. The other apostles looked at him questioningly.

`` `There can be no doubt that the tomb is open,' he said. `And I saw no evidence of Jesus inside other than the discarded graveclothes. No angels appeared to me, but they have delivered their message, so they probably have no need to remain.

``Jesus appeared to us that evening when we were at dinner.

``We had just sat down when there was a knock on the door. Not just any knock but an insistent knock. Cleopas and David were there, looking agitated.

`` `What is it?' Peter asked. `Have you heard that the tomb is empty? Do you know anything?'

`` `Of course the tomb is empty!' Cleopas exclaimed. `Jesus is risen! We saw him this morning!' And he told us his story - if you want to hear it, you should ask him for it - it is again a fascinating tale!

``But he was in the middle of this, and we were starting to break bread when there was a sudden hush, like an intake of breath, and all the candles fluttered. And then ... and then ... then, there he was! Jesus himself, in our midst!

``And he was there in his body - like Paul preaches - the bodily resurrection of Jesus was accomplished. And he sat down with us as he had so often before and shared our meal of bread and fish and honey. And then he explained everything to us - and everything he had ever said suddenly became clear, almost as if somebody had lifted a veil from over our eyes.

``And then he disappeared as mysteriously as he had appeared. And I think you know the rest.

``But that day will always stand out as the most amazing experience of my life - even compared to the day he healed me!''
