9. Kerygma in Prison

AD 51 Philippi

18 years after the crucifixion

``Who dares to accuse us, when God has already chosen us to be his messengers? The judge of all the world has himself declared us free from sin.

``Who then dares to set himself up in a position to condemn? Only Christ could even think to do that, but it was Christ himself who died for us, the very Christ who rose for us, and since Christ reigns in power for us, Christ prays for us!

``Who are these mere men who thus think they can come between us and the work to which we have been appointed?

``I ask you, my friends, is it possible that anything can come between us and the love of Christ? Can trouble, pain or persecution? Can lack of clothes and food, danger to life and limb, the threat of force of arms?

``No, my friends, it is not possible. We should every day that in all these things we win an overwhelming victory through Christ who has proved his love for us through his death and resurrection.

``And I am absolutely convinced that neither death nor life, no angels or demons, not the emperor himself, the events of today or tomorrow, nor anything else you could possibly name has the power to separate us from the love of God in Jesus Christ our Lord!

``How could it be otherwise, my friends? Consider, if you will, a world in which God was not the most powerful being. How would that even work? If the emperor could tell God, the God who made the world and everything in it, what to do? How would that make sense? Surely the world would come to an end!

``But, you say, here we are, the elect of God, locked up in a Roman Prison! Is God treating us unjustly?

``Never! God said long ago to Moses: 'I will have mercy on whomever I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whomever I will have compassion'.

``It is obviously not a question of human will or human effort, but of divine mercy. The scripture says to Pharaoh: 'Even for this same purpose I have raised you up, that I might show my power in you, and that my name shall be declared in all the earth' .

``It seems plain, then, that God chooses on whom he will have mercy, and whom he will harden in their sin.

``You may retort: "If this is so, and God's will is irresistible, why does God blame men for what they do?" But the question really is this: "Who are you, a man, to make any such reply to God?" When a craftsman makes anything he doesn't expect it to turn round and say, 'Why did you make me like this?'

``The potter, for instance, is always assumed to have complete control over the clay, making with one part of the lump a lovely vase, and with another a pipe for sewage. Can we not assume that God has the same control over human clay? May it not be that God, though he must sooner or later expose his wrath against sin and show his controlling hand, has yet most patiently endured the presence in his world of things that cry out to be destroyed? Can we not see, in this, his purpose in demonstrating the boundless resources of his glory upon those whom he considers fit to receive his mercy, and whom he long ago planned to raise to glorious life? And by these chosen people I mean you and me, whom he has called out from both Jews and Gentiles. He says in Hosea: 'I will call them my people, who were not my people, and her beloved, who was not beloved'. 'And it shall come to pass in the place where it was said to them, You are not my people, there they will be called sons of the living God'.

``And Isaiah, speaking about Israel, proclaims: 'though the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, the remnant will be saved. For he will finish the work and cut it short in righteousness, because the Lord will make a short work upon the earth'.

``Let us have no imitation Christian love. Let us have a genuine break with evil and a real devotion to good.

``Let us have real warm affection for one another as between brothers, and a willingness to let the other man have the credit.

``Let us not allow slackness to spoil our work and let us keep the fires of the spirit burning, as we do our work for God.

``Base your happiness on your hope in Christ. When trials come endure them patiently, steadfastly maintain the habit of prayer.

``Give freely to fellow-Christians in want, never grudging a meal or a bed to those who need them ... as for those who try to make your life a misery, bless them. Don't curse, bless.

``Share the happiness of those who are happy, the sorrow of those who are sad.

``And remember, never take vengeance into your own hands, my dear friends: stand back and let God punish if he will. For it is written: 'Vengeance is mine. I will repay'.

``These are God's words: 'Therefore if your enemy hungers, feed him; if he thirsts, give him a drink; for in so doing you will heap coals of fire on his head'. Don't allow yourself to be overpowered with evil. Take the offensive - overpower evil by good!

``And remember, that God always answers our earnest prayers, often even before we ask. So let us now give thanks unto the Lord most high, eternal in the heavens who will bring our deliverance this very evening.

``My brothers, let us sing from the Psalms ...''
