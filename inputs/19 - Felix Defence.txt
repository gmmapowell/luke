@Chapter
title=Felix Defence

&date AD56 Caesarea
&date 23 years after the crucifixon

Tertullus was a Jewish lawyer versed in Roman law.

``Your excellency, Governor Felix: we owe it to you personally,
that we Jews here enjoy lasting peace, and we know that it is due
to your foresight that our nation currently enjoys improved living
conditions compared to past times.

``While we acknowledge these things with the deepest gratitude, I 
must beg you to give us a brief hearing with your customary kindness.
The simple fact is that we have found this man a pestilential
disturber of the peace among the Jews all over the world. He
is a ringleader of the Nazarene sect, and he was on the point
of desecrating the Temple when we overcame him. But you yourself
will soon discover from the man himself all the facts about which
we are accusing him.''

Felix was a typically rapacious Roman governor, who saw every
situation through a single lens: how could it enrich him personally.

``His desecration of your Temple is neither here nor there as far
as I am concerned, except inasmuch as his actions disturb the
peace.  And yet, by your own account not his actions, but yours,
disturbed the peace.  We have had words about this before: your
religion is at odds with the Pax Romana and if it cannot bend, we
will have to break it.  The recommendation I have is to release
him and, if I understand all parties correctly, he will leave
this province and go back to his home province which, again, if
I have the correct information - is Cilicia.  This would seem to
suit all parties well, since he cannot `defile' your Temple if he
is five hundred miles away ...''

``Your excellency, this man is a danger wherever he may go.  The
creed he spouts undermines the Pax Romana by preaching an alternative
emperor who wishes to usher in a new Kingdom sweeping away the existing
order and replace it with his own order with this man he claims to
follow - Jesus of Nazareth - as the uncrowned emperor of the entire
world.''

``But am I not right in thinking that you, too, pray for the day
when your `God' will come down from his habitation and rip up the
world order and replace it with his own Kingdom?  In this sense
are you and he not on the same side?  What then is your point of
dissent?''

``It is true, your excellency, that we look for the times when our
God will come and cause all men to repent of their sins and come
home to righteousness and bring light and mercy to all men.  But that
does not mean that we wish to support a violent overthrow of the
emperor.  We accept the separate domains of state and religion and
the modus vivendi where you handle the profane affairs of our people
and we deal with their spiritual welfare and religious activities.

``And while we continue to accept those who seek out a deeper
faith and connection to the Mysteries of the World, we do not
aggressively try to undermine the fabric of society in the way
that this man does and subvert law-abiding Romans to become
freeloaders and debauchers.''

``So, this is your charge against this man: that he is trying to
subvert the fabric of Roman society.  For his own ends?  Or for
others?  In short, remember the great sobriquet of the lawyer Cicero:
cui boni?  Who benefits from his disruption of society?  If you
cannot answer that, I say with the Great Advocate: `you have no case
and I move to dismiss with prejudice'.''

``Your excellency is quite astute.  That is indeed the most
compelling aspect of the whole matter.  This man is one of the
most self-possessed and arrogant men you will meet.  Of course,
that in itself is no crime.  Many men are such.

``But when you couple it with his actions, it is clear that his
motivation is self-aggrandizement and the man that benefits is
this man himself as he draws to himself the power and prestige
that comes from his setting himself up as the head of this Jesus
cult and drawing to him and putting under his power all those
poor, weak, defenceless souls who hear his sublime preaching -
beware the magic in his speech when he conducts his defence,
your excellency; his rhetoric is up there with that of the
Great Advocate himself! - he intends to create a force that,
spread across the entire empire, will one day receive the message
to overthrow the emperor and his cohorts.

``If not stopped, this man will overturn the entire empire.''

``A serious charge indeed.  You accuse this man, then of the
crime of `Hostis' - of serious treason and being an enemy of
the empire.  Such a charge, if proved, would of course carry
the death penalty, served immediately.  This would no doubt
suit your ends, but what proof can you offer of such a charge?''

``Your excellency.  The proof is all there for the seeing.
You will hear it from his own lips when you cross-question him
in his defence.  He openly preaches that the end times are
here; that his leader Jesus, who was himself executed for treason by
your predecessor Pontius Pilate, has been brought back to life
by God, and that he is just waiting in the wings to sweep back
into power, displacing all legitimate authorities.

``That, in the process of preaching this, he has gathered
large communities around Asia and in Greece, mainly drawn from
Jews and fellow-travellers, who subscribe to his viewpoint
and will follow his commands implicitly, and all of whom
are implacably opposed to accepting the overlordship of the
Roman government system.

``In short, he has the motivation of setting himself - or this
Jesus if he is truly alive - as the supreme ruler of the
world; he has the capability through the believers he has
subverted; and he has the opportunity presented through the
resentment felt by many around the empire against his excellency
the Emperor and his government.''

``Granted.  There is clearly a case here which needs to be
investigated and answered.  It is also a case which, given
that it involves both a charge of Hostis and an alleged 
Empire-wide conspiracy, may only really be tried in Rome.  It
may be that our verdict will be that he will be sent to Rome
for trial in front of the Emperor.  In that case, if you wish
to pursue the case, you will need to find the resources to
take your prosecution in front of the Emperor.  I tell you
this now because you will know as I do of the ruinous expense
of such a course of action.  If you wish to admit now that
this is simply a malicious prosection and drop it, I will
let you go with no more than a warning to stay away from
my courts in future.  But if you proceed, you will need
to stay the course, however long, drawn-out and expensive
it may be.  Do you understand?  Do you wish to reconsider?''

``No, we do not, your excellency.  This man is a danger to
all of us and we cannot cease prosecuting him any more than
you would be able to cease serving the Emperor faithfully.''

``Very good.  So, Paul, eminent citizen of Tarsus in Cilicia,
what defence do you have in answer to these charges of Hostis,
the most serious crime in the Roman Law Code?''

``As much as my opponent in this case, I am well aware that you
have been governor of this nation for many years, and of the peace,
prosperity and justice that you have brought us.  I feel no
reservation therefore in putting the whole of my case in front of
you and feel that the facts speak for themselves.

``Since I landed in this port and started to Jerusalem not more
that twelve days ago, you can easily verify with the harbour master,
the citizens with whom I lodged and others with whom I met that I was
here and did not in any way disrupt the peace.

``You can then send to Jerusalem and make enquiries there.  I will
provide your agents with all the details of places I stayed, people
I met and the actions I took.  You will see that I have kept a low
profile both here and there.

``I was never found arguing with anyone, either in the Temple in Jerusalem,
or in the synagogues here, or anywhere else.  Neither will you find
that I was gathering crowds together in any location.  If I was truly
trying to gather personal followers for a revolution, would I not do that?

``Likewise, if I was trying to gather an army, would I not be found meeting
with subversive types and secretly recruiting them?  I defy your agents -
or my accusers', for that matter - to find any evidence or witnesses who
will support the charges they are now making against me.  At least, you
will not find any who are still prepared to do so when you threaten them
with scourging if you catch them in a lie.

``I will freely admit to you, however, that I do worship the God of our
fathers according to the Way which my accusers call a heresy, although
in fact I believe in the scriptural authority of both the Law and the Prophets,
even as they do.  We believe in the same God, the same scriptures and the
same prophecy of the End Times. I have the same hope in God which they
themselves hold, that there is to be a resurrection of both good men
and bad in those final days. With this hope before me I do my utmost
to live my whole life with a clear conscience before God and man.

``So, you ask, if not revolution, what would it be that would make me
come to Judea?  As you wisely note, I am a citizen of Tarsus and Cilicia
and have spent the past few years in Greece.  It has in fact been many
years since I last visited our Temple in Jerusalem.  Well, I will tell you
the truth, and it is so not sinister that it may even be suspicious.

``As governor, you cannot be ignorant of the issues with drought and famine
that have afflicted your province.  And I am aware of many of the remedial
actions which you have taken to try and alleviate the suffering of your
people.  And I know that all of those who have benefitted from your wise
rule appreciate your efforts and your forethought.

``Well, in my own small way, I was about much the same charitable work.
Our brothers in the Way in Asia and Greece had collected a small amount of
money which they dedicated to the purpose of buying provisions for the
benefit of starving brothers here in Judea.  As a man absent for many
years, I felt it incumbent upon me to go to the Temple of my Fathers
and to discharge a vow of personal service that I had made some months
ago.  In doing that, I went with a number of brothers who had made similar
vows for deliverance from debt.  Since I was the vehicle of their discharge,
we chose to go to the Temple together.''

``So you freely admit that you brought money here to buy friends and
followers?''

``I freely admit that I brought money from Asia to Judea.  As far as I
am aware that is not a crime.  Trophimus - from Ephesus - has all the records
of the monies received and disbursed.  He is here in my party today.  You
can obtain from him all the records; they are all open, nothing is hidden.
You can then send your agents to track down those who received the money -
along with those in Asia who gave it, if so be your desire - and enquire of
them of my motivations and the terms of my gift.

``It was in the middle of these religious duties in the Temple that
my accusers found me, a man purified in the Temple.  I did not raise
a mob and I did not start a disturbance.  There was, in short, nothing
happening except a few men going about their religious duties in the
Template until some Jews from Asia came and attacked me.

``In my opinion, if they had a legitimate charge to lay against me,
they should have come before you and made their accusation.  But wait!
At that time, as far as I can tell, the charges they lay against me
had nothing to do with the Emperor!  They did not accuse me of inciting
treason, but of defiling the Temple.

``Now, what, you may ask, does it mean to defile the Temple?  First and
foremost, I was accused of taking a Gentile into the inner courts of the
Temple.  Now, when we say `Gentile', I think we can all agree that the
Emperor, may he have a long life!, would be classified as Gentile, not
Jew.  So if I had taken the Emperor into the Temple, that too, would
have been defiling the Temple, and they would have stoned him.  Am I
not right?  You know the story of how Pompey came into the Temple with
his guards searching for gold - and how the priests of the day screamed
sacrilege and would have stoned him.

``In which case, surely, it is they who would be guilty of Hostis, not me.
Or are we now saying that it is acceptable to stone Roman citizens -
of any rank - without trial?

``So what it comes down to is that they are clearly aggrieved at me
for some reason, but not any of the reasons that they have stated.  It
cannot be that I am plotting against the Empire - for a start, I'm
not - and we have established that if anyone were plotting against the
Empire, it would be them.

``The competent Colonel Lysias in charge of the Jerusalem garrison
investigated the charges and sent his report to you along with my
person.  When he interviewed me, he was left confused as to what
even the charge was against me.  You are surely not suggesting that
such a one would be confused about the charges to be levelled against
one who was actively plotting against Rome?  No!  He would have
empanelled a jury immediately and I would have been crucified as
an example before the end of the day!

``If he had felt there would be lesser criminal charges to be levelled
against me, he would again have put me on trial himself, or else -
as he has eventually done - referred the case to his superiors.

``But in fact his first action was not any of those.  He convoked
the Jewish religious court and took me for trial before them.  Why?
Because the _only_ crimes he could see that I could possibly be
guilty of were religious ones under the Jewish faith, and those are
`crimes' which do not come under his competence.  In other words,
he recognized that I had done nothing criminal under Roman law, and
this was purely a local, religious matter, for which the Jewish
religious court could recommend lesser sentences but not confinement
or the death penalty.

``This then, is what happened in front of the Sanhedrin.  I was
accused of defiling the Temple.  I provided my defence - a simple one,
that I did not - and they did not have, and could not produce, any
credible evidence that I did.  And that I was on trial for one thing,
and one thing only, which was to preach that the God of Israel had
shown, through the infallible proof of raising a man from the dead,
that he had come in his power to show us the way forward in Truth.

``You see these men arrayed in front of you, accusing me?  This is
not the whole of the Sanhedrin.  Reasonable enough, you may say; it
is an expensive business sending dignitaries across the country and
one or two can do the job of thirty.

``True enough, but in the same way that the Roman Republic had its
_boni_, _optimates_ and _populares_, we Jews have Sadducees and
Pharisees.  All these men are Sadducees?  So what, I hear you ask.

``I am a Pharisee.

``As a Pharisee, I believe that in the final days all men will be
raised bodily from the grave.  Now that body will not be the same
body that is put in the ground, any more than the wheat which you
harvest is the same wheat you plant as seed.  But we will be raised
up in bodies incorruptible before our God.  And I believe that all
men, both good and bad, will be raised up to appear before God as
I appear before you.  And then all will be judged.

``As Sadducees, they believe none of this.

``So what?  A trifling religious difference.  Not so if I preach -
and I do! - that one has already led the way by appearing amongst
us, bodily resurrected by God.  If true - and it is! - then it
completely undercuts their position that this is _simply not possible_.

``For, if one man be raised from the dead, _how is it possible
to say there is no resurrection of the dead_?

``And thus I must be suppressed.  By violence - they tried that
in the Temple; by plotting - they tried that as I came here;
or by outright lying about my actions - as they do in front of
you today.

``Here I rest my defence, and request an immediate verdict
of _ABSOLVO_.''
