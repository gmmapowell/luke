@Chapter
title=Caesarea

&date AD56 Caesarea
&date 23 years after the crucifixion

Paul's defence was masterful to watch.

He had been dragged into the judgment hall in chains,
and lined up opposite half a dozen members of the Sanhedrin,
all of whom, Timothy told me, were part of the Sadducee party
who had insisted on Paul's guilt when the Sanhedrin had 
split on party lines at Paul's previous trial.

He was looking a bit the worse for wear after a week
in detention, but the fire was still burning deep down inside
those eyes that I remembered so well from the first time I had
heard him preach: the burning passion of the Spirit within him.

They had organized their case cleverly, well knowing that a Roman
governor would be not the least interested in Jewish theology,
argued that Paul was plotting against the Empire.  A charge no
governor could possibly ignore, and many would have taken the
safer option of just executing him `in case'.

Felix allowed them to make their case, prosecuted by their
patron Tertullus who, although to the Jews was `just a lawyer',
was, by dint of being a Roman citizen, outranked all of them
in the eyes of a regularly-formed Roman court.

He was well-versed in the forms of Roman law and practice, and
presented probably the strongest case that could be presented
against Paul in the Premio Actio.  He argued that Paul had
caused a riot and was plotting the overthrow of the Empire.

But Paul's defence - by harping on about the petty jealousies
at play, and downplaying the number of riots which he had
caused over his career - managed to make it sound like a very
minor religious squabble.  And advanced a long line of witnesses
for the Governor to consider in his defence.

When Paul called for the immediate verdict after the Premio Actio
- in other words, suggesting that his opponents had so failed
to make their case that they should not be allowed to proceed,
I could almost see him itching to do so.  Romans are very
efficient with their justice - not always just, perhaps, but
very efficient - and to deal with the case promptly by dismissing
the charges and then banning Paul from ever returning to Judea
seemed very appealing to Felix.

But more appealing to Felix than justice was money.  And he had
a good nose for where there was money to be made in court cases.

Obviously the practice of bribing officials for verdicts was
illegal; and therefore extremely widespread.  This case had all
the elements of a valuable case: the plaintiffs clearly had
funds; the defendant appeared to be well-connected too.  Nobody
would really criticize Felix if he overreacted and put a man
to death if he might be plotting against the Empire; and yet
the evidence was so flimsy as to be non-existent and so an
acquittal would also be a completely reasonable verdict.  In
such a situation, a bidding war would decide the outcome.

Eventually Felix spoke.

``I cannot at this time see that the prosecution has a case,
and I am tempted to move as the prisoner requests for an
instant dismissal; especially since, as he points out, that
was also the verdict of my Colonel in Jerusalem who was on
the scene at the time of the original incident.

``At the same time, these charges - although unsubstantiated
- are of a very serious nature.  I would not like to be
the man who let Spartacus escape.

``I am going to adjourn this trial for a week.  In the meantime,
I will send for Colonel Lysias and some of the other witnesses
whom you have named on both sides.  When they arrive, we will
begin the Segundo Actio.  But be warned!  Your evidence had
better be compelling against this man, for I am well aware of
your dissentions and divisions, and if your only charge against
this man transpires to be that he is a member of this Jesus-sect
you are going to make me very angry!  And you do not want to be
dealing with an angry governor for the next year or for however
long the Senate continues to prorogue me!''

--

One of the reasons Felix knew so much about the internal disputes
between the various sects of the Jews, including the Way, was
that he had married a Jewess whose esteemed father had been made a Roman
citizen for services to the Empire and had taken one of the then-Emperor's
names in respect and homage and become `Drusus'.  In accordance with
Roman tradition his daughter Rebecca was known in Roman circles as
`Drusilla', in much the same way that Saul was known as Paul or Simon
the Apostle was known as Peter.

When she had heard about the trial of Paul, she remarked that she had
heard much of this man and his preaching of holiness and expressed
a desire to hear his defence.  Although contrary to normal Roman
practice, women were not actually _barred_ from appearing in a
court, and Felix was able - as he had in times past - to argue that
her expert knowledge, combined with lack of bias - made her an
invaluable aid to him in getting to the bottom of the case.

When the week was up, and the various witnesses had presented themselves
to Felix' clerk, the trial was resumed.

--

``I say again, that the only `crime' for which I am being persecuted
is the crime of saying out loud that God loves us, God has forgiven
us, and that he sent Jesus into the world to suffer for us, to die
for us, and then to rise again to foreshadow for us the things that
are yet to come.

``Let us be clear.  I am not saying that here and now anybody should
rise up and oppose the Empire.  But all things come to an end, and
the times of this world are passing away and the end times are
coming when we shall all be resurrected and brought in front of
God for judgment as I appear before you today.  In those days, there
will not be Romans and non-Romans, Greeks and Jews, Metics and Persians.
We will all just be people in front of God.  And when called to the
bar of justice, what will we answer?

``There are many possible answers.

``One may say `I did as I pleased,
without reference to Law or Man, and I stand today as I lived,
confident in my power and glory.'  Woe to that man in that day, for
he shall see what it means to live by power.

``A Jew may say, `From my youth, I have kept the law.  I have
fasted on fast days, feasted on feast days, eaten the things approved,
avoided the things not approved, kept myself free from pollution,
and stoned the defiler.'  That man must be careful in that day,
for while the outward appearance may be of piety, God does not
look simply on the outward appearance as man does, but will look
deep inside him and will know the contents of his heart.

``And God will ask him, `and did you love your fellow-man?'  And
when he confidently says, `Yes, Lord!' the Lord will show unto him
images from his life: Gentiles spurned, corrupt flesh unhealed,
the poor ignored, in order that he might maintain his purity
and status.  Woe, I say, unto that man in the day of judgment!

``But what will I say in that day?  I will say with humility,
`many errors have I made, O God, many sins have I committed, for
which I ask your mercy.  But I have seen the One whom you sent,
and I have seen what it is to live a life of goodness and mercy,
and while I may have failed, I have tried to follow in the
footsteps of He that you sent.  I beg your forgiveness.'  Will
I be saved?  I believe so, but until _that day_ none of us can
truly know what will happen.

``You ask me of my preaching.  It is very simple.  I will summarize
for you.

``Throughout the ages, men have come who have seen the plan of
God.  I am sure this is the same in Rome as it is in Greece and
as it is in Judea.  It is to our credit that while we have
generally despised such men, we have listened to them.

``God sends these men to tell us his plan and to make the world
a better place, in the same way that the Emperor sends governors
to the provinces to regulate their affairs.

``But the prophets in Judea do not come with political power,
or with prestige, or with wealth.  They are poor men, who wander
the deserts and the wilderness preaching what they know to whoever
will listen.  Since those who most want to hear the message are
the poor and the needy, large groups follow such men.  Men with
the symbols of this world are understandably afraid of such followings,
thinking that surely revolution will follow in their wake.

``In our time, one came amongst us who had all the properties
of the old-fashioned prophet, but with one added feature: he
came directly from God to send us a `final notice of eviction'.
God's message was this: `wise up now or face the final judgment
unprepared'.  And that judgment is coming now.

``Of this man the powerful and privileged were afraid.  I
shudder to say it, but I was one of those men.  I persecuted
his followers when they came to Antioch where I lived at the
time.  And by plotting against him - as these men now plot
against me - they were able to convince Pontius Pilate to 
crucify him!

``But God could not tolerate that his Great Messenger should
be done to death!  And God reached into that lonely tomb and
raised him up alive!  And not just alive, alive in his own
body!

``And in this body of resurrection he appeared to many who had
known him before his crucifixion - and others, as myself, who
had not personally known him.  And those who believed, believed
with all their heart, that this man was sent by God as his
personal messenger were changed!  In a heartbeat, as they saw
the reality that was God's Truth, all these men were infused
with the Holy Spirit.

``And in that heartbeat, your entire outlook changes.  Petty
concerns - for status, wealth or power - all seem bankrupt
before the grand vision of God's design.  You have no choice
but to give up such feeble pursuits - the hoarding of wealth;
the jealous guarding of power - and move on out into the world
to preach the coming Kingdom of God and the need for all
men to repent _now_, while there is still time, to give
up their wealth, power and prestige, and to accept with
humility the instruction of God.

``And since that day ...''

``Enough!'' snapped Felix, obviously agitated, in part on his own
account, but more so on the effect he saw in his wife's face.
``This trial is adjourned again.  You are to be placed under house
arrest with Philip, a known member of your Jesus-sect in this
city and to be available at any moment to return here for
further investigation.

``I am clear in my own mind that you are not unreasonably plotting
against the Empire - and cannot find you guilty of Hostis - but
I fear this new doctrine which you preach.  I will consider and
gather further evidence.

``Adjourned!!''
