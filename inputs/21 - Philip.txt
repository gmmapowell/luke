@Chapter
title=Philip

&date AD56 Caesarea
&date 23 years after the crucifixion

At least Paul was out of the prison cell.

As was normal with Roman justice, Paul, as a well-to-do
citizen of some standing, was not confined to the public
cells while on remand, but was lodged with a reputable
member of society, in this case Philip.  Officially,
Philip was responsible for Paul's confinement - and also
his welfare - but in practice the system worked not 
by relying on any kind of force or restraint, but by depending
on the desire of the incarcerated individual to protect
the reputation of his `patron' - the man who stood up
for him in court.

There was no requirement for such a remanded man to earn
a living - he was forbidden to leave the house after all -
but for Paul the idea of being a burden on a family of limited
resources and dedicated to spreading the word of God was
anathema.  He insisted on picking up a trade and rapidly found
out what work was being done in the brotherhood and had rapidly
attached himself to a family of tentmakers responsible for
kitting out the Syrian legions with marching tents.  These
two legions, facing the Parthians across the Euphrates,
constituted about ten thousand men of all ranks and responsibilities.
Each decury of ten men shared a tent, a grand total of a thousand
tents, and not of any small size.  Of these, usually a couple
of hundred needed replacing in any given season, and others
were brought in for repair.  Much of the work was handled out of
the main cities of Damascus and Antioch, but in line with the
standard Imperial practice of trying to keep everybody in the
Empire happy, work was spread out around the province.

Thus Paul was able to bring in a small income and pay Philip
for his lodging.  And he commented that, as prisons went, and
he felt he was something of an expert, this was one of the
best he had encountered.

For the first few weeks, I was elsewhere in the city; of course,
as a doctor and not confined to Philip's house, I had no difficulty
in plying my trade, earning a living and paying for my own
lodging in order to avoid overcrowding Philip.  During this time,
Felix did not call Paul back to answer any more charges or
to challenge him over his defence.  The case had gone quiet, and,
like so many others across the Empire, it seemed that it might
just linger on until Paul died a natural death.  Surprisingly,
for a man who chafed at every delay in promulgating the Word,
Paul seemed remarkably calm, as if in possession of the plan
God had laid out for him.

One day I was at dinner at Philip's house, usually a riotous
affair of preaching, glorifying God and prophesying.  But on
this occasion, it was relatively quiet and orderly.  During
a gap in the conversation, I ventured a personal question.

``Tell me, Philip, how did you become involved with the Way?''

``Well, now, that's a funny story, particularly with your friend
Paul the Persecutor at the same table,'' he said and laughed.
``It actually all started when Jesus was preaching in the hill towns
of Galilee.  I was one of `the seventy' that he saw potential in
and sent out through the towns announcing his presence in the
fields and drawing out the crowds.

``Obviously, we preached the message he brought; not in the same
way of course - what we had to say was a pale imitation of the
drama he brought to the message on those hillsides, but even so,
some of the miracles we saw happen!  I remember one time as we were
walking into a town a child fell and broke his leg.  My partner
- we always went around in pairs - just fell down to the ground,
straightened it out, and raised him up!  He did that in the
full sight of the child's parents - well, you can imagine they
came out to hear more!

``Then came that terrible week in Jerusalem.  You can't imagine
the fanfare we had drummed up by the time we entered the city!
All the prophecies were fulfilled, and everybody knew that he
was coming into the city and all the followers he had acquired
over the previous months were there shouting `Hosanna!' and waving
palm fronds they had hacked down off trees.

``Well, you can imagine how the Sanhedrin reacted.  They went
ballistic.  I mean, this was passover week and the city was
buzzing and then the rumours spread that Jesus was going to
bring in the millenium right then and there in the Temple -
he was going to sit in the Chair of David and the Kingdom of
David would come and the Romans would be expelled ...

``The leaders of the Sanhedrin plotted together with the Roman
guards to do away with him.  Their plans nearly fell through when
the Governor decided that, whatever he might be accused of, it
was not really his intention to overthrow the legitimate Roman
government.

``But then, of course, it really backfired when, after that miserable
weekend when we all believed that Jesus was dead and the whole
experience had been an illusion, he came among us at dinner that
evening - alive!''

``You were there?'' I asked.  I'd known when I met Peter and James
that they had been there, but in the time I'd known Philip he'd
not mentioned that he was a _genuine_ eyewitness of the Risen Christ.
Not that I really doubted that Paul had met Jesus, but somehow he
always described it in such a way that it always felt more like 
a vision than a literal meeting.  And so much time had elapsed
by then.

``Yes, I was there.  I wasn't there at the tomb when Mary and Mary
went to finish the embalming that morning; and I wasn't there when
the apostles met to decide what to do and Mary and Mary arrived just
before he did; but then the word went out to all the followers who
hadn't left Jerusalem that he had said he would be there for the
evening meal.  And he was!''

I was floored for questions to ask.  What do you ask someone who's
just told you that he was a contemporary eye-witness of the event
that has come to define your life.  All the more with Paul there,
who had suffered so much because of that one event.  What was there
to say?  I decided to move on.

``Then what happened?''

``We saw him a few more times over the next few weeks; he seemed
to just come and go.  And others saw him too: some recognized him;
some didn't.

``And then he ascended.  I'm sure you've heard the stories about
that from Paul.  He wasn't there of course - he was still in
Antioch at the time, but he's heard all about it.  One moment he
was there, all shining and bright ... and then he was brighter ...
and then he was gone, never to be seen again - except by Paul, here.''

And that story I knew.  I wanted Philip's stories.

``Well, after that, we couldn't help but preach what we had
seen and lived.  I mean that literally; it would have been agony
to try and resist the power of the Spirit.  And that was
contagious - you've probably felt the same yourself.  When the
word is inside you, you just have to let it out.

``And so, much as the authorities tried to put a lid on it and 
suppress it, the Word just grew and grew.  They put Peter and
James in prison for a while.  But in the same way that the grave
could not hold Jesus, the prison could not hold Peter and James -
they literally just walked out one night!

``Well, you can imagine that after a while, you have a large
community all working and living together, sharing all their
material wealth and needs, and the senior group doing most of
the work of preaching the Word and demonstrating the Way,
there was a certain amount of discord about how this `shared'
wealth was being distributed.  And, time and again, issues came
up to Peter and James that they felt they couldn't really deal with:
they would quote Jesus' remark `who made me a judge among you?'
and ask them to pray and sort it out.

``But by the time there were three or four thousand of us,
that had passed the bounds of practicality.  And somebody said
to Peter that if _he_ didn't want to deal with it, he should
possibly find somebody - or somebodies - that did.

``And so they appointed seven of us to be `deacons' - judges
if you will, or arbiters.  The key point is that we were supposed
to channel the Spirit not in the growth of the Way but in
the harmony of the Way.  And we did.

``One of the other deacons was my partner from our journeys
around Galilee - Stephen was his name.  A truly amazing man.  And
I'm sure you know his story too.  Well, eventually, the rulers
plotted against him, too, and I've heard Paul say that he was in
on that plot, feeling that some of the pressure in Antioch would
be reduced if the most visible leadership in Jerusalem were
taken out.

``And so they arrested him, and stoned him.

``Paul was pleased, of course, and promptly moved on the converts
in Antioch and then moved on to Damascus to try and deal with the
growing strength of the Way there - and you know what happened
with Ananias, the leader of the Way there.

``In Jerusalem, a veritable storm broke loose.  Temple guards went
around the city to the various houses we were living in, pulling
people out in the middle of the night and locking them up.  As now,
they argued they were collaborating with the Romans, trying to
avoid a revolution.

``We decided to scatter.  The apostles stayed put; they felt that
either they were above persecution or that they would be protected
by their faith in Jesus.  As deacons, we were instructed to take
a segment of the faithful with us wherever we went and to guide them.

``I headed north, and wound up in Samaria.  To this day, you can
go there and find a group of the Way, living in peace and harmony,
who will be able to trace their presence there to that persecution.
They never returned of course; and they became the kernel of a
great outpouring of faith and preaching in that part of the world.

``Again, on the one hand, the rulers managed to achieve the objective
of breaking up the large, dangerous group of subversives in Jerusalem;
but on the other, all they succeeded in doing was spreading them out
across the whole of Judea and Samaria, and often further afield.

``And as they spread, they would win converts, and then the apostles
would come, and preach, and show the wonder of the coming Kingdom
of Heaven, and the Holy Spirit would come down and spread among the new
communities.  And so the Word spread over the whole of the country.

``And far from alleviating Paul's problems in Syria, many of the refugees
fled there.

``But, of course, by then, Paul had changed sides.''

``I'd seen the light,'' corrected Paul.

``Of course,'' said Philip.

--

It was a couple of weeks later before I could hear the next instalment
of Philip's story.  My medical practice, much like the early Way, was
growing rapidly and I felt I owed it to all those brothers with whom
my wealth was shared to take all the work I could.

But one evening I was back at Philip's house and it was relatively quiet;
most of the household had gone to bed and Philip and Paul were seated
in the garden looking at the pool.

``So why did you leave Samaria?''

``The authorities were happy enough to let the Way flourish in a limited
way; the threat came from our preachers and demonstrators who were
actually making a difference.  The potency of our preaching and demonstration
showed up what a false religion the priests were now peddling - if there
was ever any truth to it.  They tell others what they have to do, and they
make a living from their failure to do it.  The undoubted holiness of our
followers is its own defence, but for the preachers, how can we possibly avoid
criticizing the sterility of the official cult?

``So they trumped up charges against me, and tried to arrest me.  Then
one evening, much like this, I was in my house in Samaria and an angel
came to me and told me to head south to Gaza and I would meet a man
who was looking for me.

``It was a bizarre experience, and it felt much like running away.  How
was I supposed to meet this man?  It made no sense, but I had learnt
that faith made all things possible - how often I have heard that; how
often I have _said_ that; and yet how hard it is to truly _live_ it!
But I did on that occasion.

``I started making my way south, and I made it to the main coast road,
figuring that I might be able to find a good soul who would give me a
lift down to Gaza.

``And I saw a chariot come up, obviously from a wealthy man, a leader
from some nation, and I thought, it is quite impossible that this
man would help an itinerant preacher on his journey.  But the Spirit
moved me and I ran up, determined to ask him.

``And as I approached, I saw he had a scroll in his hands, and I heard
the words coming from his mouth as he read from the scroll.  And I'm sure
you're very familiar with the words.

`` `He was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb silent
before his shearer; he never opened his mouth.  Humiliated, he was
treated unjustly.  And who will recount his story?  For he was killed
and taken away.'

``I was surprised, to be sure.  One of the canonical prophecies about
Jesus, coming from this man I felt impelled to approach.  And then the
Spirit opened my eyes and I saw: this _was_ the man the Spirit wanted
me to meet!

``And the right words just issued from my mouth without any conscious
thought on my part: `You read the prophet Isaiah; do you understand
what you read?'

`` `I don't know,' he replied. `Was the prophet writing about himself?
Did he die this terrible death?  Or was it somebody else for whom he
predicted these things?'

``And so I told him.  I told him how fortunate he was that he lived
in this generation when all these prophecies had come true.  And he
listened, and the Holy Spirit spoke through me.  And he believed.

``And as we went down to Gaza through the desert, he pointed to one
of the reservoirs that the Romans had built, and said, `Is this not
water?  What is there to stop me being baptized?  I see the error
of my ways, and I hear the Good News of the Messiah.'

``And I baptized him there, and we went our separate ways.''

``And then?'' I asked.

``Many things,'' he smiled.  ``But basically, I worked my way
up the coast, preaching and healing until I came to Caesarea,
and here I have stayed, preaching the Word and nurturing our
community up to the current day.

``But if you want to know more, you should search out the others.
I hear you've met Peter, but he is generally too busy to talk
about the old days; he is mainly focused on the work.  James
is much the same.

``But if you go back to Jerusalem you can meet Mary and Mary, and
hear about The First Day.  And there are many others there who
will tell you their stories of meeting Jesus; there are some
quite spectacular stories of conversions - Mary's for a start!''

And the more I thought about it, the more I was determined to take
his advice.
