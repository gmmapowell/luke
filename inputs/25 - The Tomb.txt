@Chapter
title=The Tomb

&date AD57 Outside Jerusalem
&date 24 years after the crucifixion

I finally found the Tomb.

I'd heard so much about the Tomb and the stone over the past few
days, that I found myself wanting to see it for myself.  In
Paul's version of the story, the Tomb itself did not figure;
indeed, Mary and Joseph hardly figured.  What mattered was the
symbolism presented by the fact that Jesus was crucified but
that death could not hold him: and thus, the end times were 
upon us; the general resurrection would surely follow; we
would all be judged; and thus the time for repentance was
very short.

In the traditions I'd heard over the last few days, centred
in and around Jerusalem, the actual event of the resurrection
was not reduced to symbolism but was remembered for the event
which it was in its own right.  And that seemed to centre
largely on the events of one Sunday morning at the Tomb.

I obviously couldn't go back in time to that Sunday morning
and observe what happened myself, much as I would have liked
to have been there; but I could visit the tomb.  I'd asked
my hosts where it was and they described it to me.  It was,
after all, Joseph's tomb and since Jesus had not taken up
permanent residence, he planned again to use it for himself,
having transcended the belief that a `fresh' tomb was somehow
holier than one that had been used in the past.

I had been told that it might be difficult to find, and my hosts
had offered to loan me a messenger as guide, but something in
me had wanted to be there alone at the moment when I saw the
tomb - much as Mary had been.  After an hour of fruitless
searching, though, I'd begun to wonder if the guide might
not have been a good idea.

But once I found the tomb, all thoughts that I should have brought
a guide vanished.  It sat there, in a small hollow, easily missed
in the overall landscape.  So quiet and unassuming for a site that
had seen the world's greatest mystery acted out.  The round stone
lay across the entrance to the cave, just as described by both
Mary and Joseph.  I walked up to it.

The grave had been resealed - presumably shortly after the resurrection -
and the stone now blocked access into the tomb.  I tried to roll
it, but that was very much beyond me.  It would have been beyond Mary
or Joseph as well, as they'd described to me.  That's why they'd
brought a number of young servants with them that morning.

I sat down on a nearby rock in the shade and studied the Tomb,
trying to see if I could make it reveal its secrets - or maybe
waiting to see if an angel would appear and give me a message.

Nothing happened.

Eventually I stood up and walked back into the city.  To paraphrase
Elijah, `there was a tomb, but the Lord was not in the tomb'.

Maybe Paul was right not to focus on the event of the resurrection -
what mattered was what we did with it.
