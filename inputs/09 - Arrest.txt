@Chapter
title=Arrest

&date AD 51 Philippi
&date 18 years after the crucifixion

The girl cried out: "These men are servants of the most high God,
and they are telling you the way of salvation."

This wasn't the first time this had happened.  And it was starting
to become a little irritating, even if I had to acknowledge that
it was true.

Paul generally did a good job of ignoring this sort of
thing.  I think he was used to it from his previous
travels and, moreover, I think he suspected that she
was something of a fraud: that she had, in short, been
paid to say this by our detractors in the city.

It was the Sabbath, and we were on our way to the riverside when
Paul finally snapped.  She was walking behind
us with her minders, and calling out her usual refrain, when
one of the members of the crowd around us addressed her.

``Are you truly able to see into the other realm?'' he asked her.

Paul turned as if burnt.

``Of course she isn't,'' he snapped.  ``It's simply the spirit
of the devil in her trying to prey on the minds of the simple
and wayward to draw them away from the true path of holiness.''

``If that's so,'' countered the man, ``and you are the
followers of Jesus, who was known for casting out these kinds,
why don't you do something about it?''

I could see the leashed expression on Paul's face.  He wanted
to retort, but knew that arguments like this could not be
easily won.  Instead he turned to the girl.

``Do you wish to be cured?'' he asked her gently.

She looked up at him, a strange expression on her face, almost
curious, as if underneath the confident exterior expressed by
the evil spirit the girl's true spirit shyly looked out at him.
She smiled ever so slightly and thinly.

Paul observed her for a few moments more.

``In that case,'' he said, talking to her, or possibly beyond
her, ``if the girl has spoken, you must obey.  I command you
in the name of Jesus who is Christ to come out of her!''

The girl's face contorted momentarily and then relaxed.  Where
before the girl's expression had generally been harsh and 
contemptuous, it now returned to the lines you would expect of
a little girl.  Her masters would take no more advantage of her.

--

At the riverside, our weekly prayer meeting was in full swing.

Silas had read the opening readings and Paul was expounding
the scriptures in his own inimitable fashion.  He assured me
that the Jews of Philippi were relatively tolerant to allow
him to keep preaching so long: in most places he had been in
Asia by now he would have been excluded from the synagogue.

It was probably helped by the ever-growing number of converts
he had recruited over the weeks.  A significant number of the
Jews in the audience - and just as many Gentiles - had become
part of the `Paul party' and staunchly defended him against
the attacks of `the traditionalists'.  But it was not clear
how much longer this would be able to continue.

Paul was in the middle of describing the acts of Jesus - and
I was listening carefully in order to later commit his words
to paper exactly as he recited them - when a group of armed
men approached from the town.

``Silence!'' the centurion in front shouted.  ``We have warrants
here for the arrest of Saul of Tarsus, commonly called Paul,
ahd his associate Silas of Antioch.  Where are they?''

As I looked at them I saw that one of the prophet-girl's
handlers was with them and, with a sinking heart, I knew what
had happened.  Disappointed at their inability to gain from the
poor girl's suffering, they had decided their only course of
action was to have Paul arrested and then to try and extort
money from him through the courts or the notoriously corrupt
provincial justice system.

Paul stood up at once, and walked towards them.  Silas
copied his example, stood and followed him to where the soldiers
were standing.  Unused to this - most miscreants tended to
run when faced with arrest - the centurion looked at him
uncertainly.

``You are Paul?''

``I am.  And this is my associate, Silas of Antioch,'' he
replied with more than a touch of mockery.  ``And you
are arresting us for the crime of healing a girl sick
with an evil spirit, acting in accord with your corrupt lusts.''
This last being directed at the girl's handler.  ``I pray
that your eyes may be opened to the truth before it is too
late.  Shall we go?''

If the centurion was surprised before, he was astounded now.
Paul seemed to _want_ to be arrested and taken to jail.  Did
he truly not know what awaited him there?

As if able to read his thoughts - and possibly he was; almost
nothing surprised me about Paul any more - Paul answered him
aloud.

``Of course I know what the inside of a Roman jail looks
like.  Every few months some malcontent or another arranges
for me to be incarcerated out of petty spite or jealousy.
The capacity for human lust for power has long since
ceased to surprise me.

``But what is surprising is the outcome I foresee.  For I
will suffer but transiently, but in the morning - you will
be a new man.''

``What do you mean by that?'' retorted the centurion.  ``Are
you threatening me?''

``I carry none of the world's weapons,'' replied Paul softly
but firmly and raising his arms wide to show he was unarmed.
``But there is a power that exceeeds anything you or I are
capable of.  When you see that power, you will yield to it,
as I had to, for there really is no choice.  A blind man
may not see the light, but once cured of his blindness, can
he really go back?''

The centurion realized he was losing this metaphysical battle
and dragged Paul and Silas off to jail.

--

The riverside meeting broke up shortly after.

Some of us - our party, obviously, but many of the other
converts as well as many of those who had not been converted -
were distressed by the morning's events and wanted to leave
quickly to find out what had become of Paul and Silas.

Others were merely aggravated by having had their meeting
disrupted but felt, more or less openly, that Paul had had it
coming by his insistent manner of teaching and harping on
about the resurrection and the coming days.  Indeed, some
openly admitted that had he not been reported to the
authorities by somebody else, they might well have done
it themselves.

We walked back to the city hurriedly, and Lydia dispatched
one of the slaves to find out what had happened to Paul and
Silas while the rest of us returned to the house to
plan our next move.

The slave reported soon afterwards.  It had been fairly
easy to track Paul an Silas' whereabouts: they had been
taken to the jail in the square and confined to custody
there until such time as the city's duumvirs could hear
their case, probably in two days' time.

In the meantime, we had figured a plan for this, the most
likely eventuality.  We would gather together lamps and
food and go and camp out in the city square by the jail,
both to show solidarity with Paul and to be in contact
with him.

The nature of the jail was such that, while completely
secure, its outer walls fronted onto the main square.
The windows, placed high up in the walls, were barred
to prevent entrance or exit, and the heavily studded
gates were barred from the inside.

By camping outside, we would be able to communicate with
Paul, albeit only if we or he were to shout and provided
there was no background noise.

The moment the slave had finished his report, Lydia
rattled off her orders.  She, herself, was going to go
and find the duumvirs and see if she could explain
the situation and head off any possible trouble and meet
us there later.

We all went about our appointed tasks.

In the meantime, Paul would have to look after himself.
