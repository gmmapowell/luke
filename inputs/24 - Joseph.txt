@Chapter
title=Joseph

&date AD57 Bethany
&date 24 years after the crucifixion

Joseph had been on my list before Mary mentioned him.

Philip had said I needed to speak to Joseph for a number of reasons,
but most importantly because he had been one of the `supporters' as
the early financial and political backers of Jesus were known.  The
crucifixion was the end-game of many plots to remove Jesus from the
political scene, but most of the others had been obstructed or defused
by quiet political maneouvring behind the scenes.

Joseph had been one of the earliest such political operatives,
supporting Jesus since he had heard him preach in the first weeks
after his baptism by John on the banks of the Jordan.

By definition, such men were incredibly well connected, and Joseph
was certainly that.  He was a member of the Jewish High Council - 
the one that ultimately put Jesus to death, but Philip had told me
that he had opposed that during the critical weeks when Jesus came
closer to Jerusalem, and had been deliberately excluded from 
`the Passover Conference' when the members of the Sanhedrin who
wanted Jesus dead met in secret so as to approach Pilate with a
`unanimous' verdict of revolution and death from the Jewish
authorities.  Such a perspective from the inside seemed fascinating;
but after Mary's tale I wasn't sure whether to be prepared for
something even more amazing or to accept that I had passed the
pinnacle and would now only hear lesser stories.

Joseph was now an old man, and lived outside the city, in a town called
Bethany across the Kidron river.  It was only a short walk from Jerusalem,
so I set off early in the morning to meet him at midday.

There was a small, happy community of members of the Way in
Bethany, based around the extended family of Lazarus, one of the
other early supporters of Jesus.

We met in the quiet interior garden of his house, sitting against the
south wall in the shade on an extremely hot day for the time of year.
He offered me a cool drink of water, which I readily accepted and
asked me why I'd come.  I explained that I was interesting in piecing
together the details of Jesus' life from those who'd actually met him
and felt moved by his mission in a personal way, and I'd heard that
he had been involved at a number of points in the story.

``True enough,'' he opened.  ``I am what many Jews often call `those
who are waiting'; my whole life, I had been waiting for the coming
of the Kingdom of God.  What Jesus made me realize was that being
a `waiter' is only really an option for the rich; the poor have to
keep on living their livesand try and be holy at the same time.  What
for me is an interesting challenge and a diversion is to them an
almost insupportable burden.  And it's not possible that God, who is
good, could have possibly intended to burden so many people in such
a way.

``But I'm telling you the story out of order.  I gained, but I also
lost.  I lost the innocence of my youth.  But let me start again.
Excuse an old man rambling.  It happens.  Too long a lawyer ...

``When I was young, my father was one of the Temple lawyers and rose
to be a member of the Temple Council.  I suppose it was in many ways
inevitable that I would follow suit, although there's no guarantee
that I would rise as high.

``My father was just old enough to remember the conquest of Pompey.
But that was his first memory - he was two or three years old at the
time - and in many ways it defined him.  He was violently opposed
to the Roman occupation his entire life.

``He always told me that I was born in the year that Octavianus
cemented his powers and took the name Augustus; and he told me that
I was born as a balance to that, to oppose that.

``But I never saw it that way.  How can you oppose the Romans?  They
are so powerful.  They have all the weapons of this world!  Violence?
They have armies the like of which have never been seen before.
Power?  They have organization and discipline unmatched in human
history.  Money?  Their Empire sucks all the gold from the four
corners of the earth into the centre, Rome.  Law?  They define
what laws are valid and if you protest, their Senate combines to
crush you.  What human weapons are there to oppose a Pompey, an
Antonius or an Augustus?  There are none.

``For a Jew, then, there is only one way out.  Radical reliance on
the God of Abraham.  And in this day and age, that really means one
thing, which is to expect him to act to bring in the promised age -
the age of his Messiah, who, the prophets reliably tell us will
sweep away the world we know and replace it with his own Kingdom;
a Kingdom not of this world, ruled by men with power and laws, but
a Kingdom of his devising, where all men are good and know him as
he is.

``What then are the signs of this new Kingdom coming?  It was to
this that I devoted myself as a young man, searching the scriptures
and looking for clues.  I met with other young men - and some
older men - who were on the same hunt.  Over time, most fell away,
absorbed with the cares of this world:  Temple rituals, marriage,
raising children.  But some of us carried on in our search.  And
became convinced that this was the era in which our prayers would
be answered.

``I was twenty-seven when I was called to the bedside of Simeon,
one of the older members of our movement.  Even older than my father,
he remembered clear back to the days of the Maccabees and the
freedom of Judea.  He had been a young man when Pompey had come
into the city and desecrated the Temple.

``And, there, on his deathbed, he told me a secret.  He had seen
the Lord's Christ, the one who had been sent to announce the
coming of the Kingdom.

``It is to begin at last,'' he said.  ``I recognized him the moment
he was placed in my hands for the blessing.  Do not seek him out,
for he must have his youth to develop and grow, but this is the
token you will take with you ... Christ is born and will come into
his own in thirty years or so.  And when he does, the sign by which
you shall know him is this: he was presented in the Temple this day.

``I went to the Temple records and noted the names of all the infant
boys who had been presented that day.  They were twenty-two in number.
I memorized their names and their parents' names.  And I waited in
earnest for the day when one of them came into their own, having faith
but never _quite_ knowing if Simeon had spoken truth, or if his desire
to see the coming of the Lord within his lifetime had determined him
to say that it _had_ happened.

``And so I waited.  From time to time, one would arise and preach of
the coming Kingdom, and I would go and listen.  And most of the time
they said nothing new, but cobbled together words and phrases from
the prophets, as any madman could, and I went home disappointed.

``Then, one day, I heard that a new man was preaching repentance of
sins by the Jordan, baptizing those who chose to renew their lives
and tormenting those who wanted easy answers.  I naturally went out
to see him, and hear what he had to say.

``The advice he gave was clear and obvious and within the parameters
of the Law.  But unlike most of the others I'd heard, his words were
clearly inspired by God.  When the crowds had drifted away at the end
of the day, baptized or dejected, I approached him and asked if
he was Christ.

`` `I?' he asked, amused. `Do I look like I have the power of the
seven kingdoms in my hands?  Do even sinners tremble before me?
Can I cure any sickness?  No, he that you seek will come after me.
I am here to pick the low-hanging fruit, to call the sick to
repentance.  I wash the outside of the body, to cleanse away the
outermost sins, but he will come and burn out your very soul with
the power of God.

`` `And yet, even so, the Kingdom will not come, because he is not
the Judge come into his Court, but still just the messenger calling
all to repentance.  But when you meet him, even you will repent of
your sins.'

``Naturally I was affronted.  What sins?  I followed the law.  I did
my duties in the Temple.  I stayed away from unclean things.  While
I did not violently oppose the Romans, I did my best to keep the 
country pure from their influence.

`` `This is the difference,' continued John, `between me and the Christ
when he comes.  He will not need to explain your sins to you.  One
glance from him, and you will know your flaws.  And then you will repent.
And now you see why so many do not choose baptism from me.  Their
arrogance and hardness of heart is too great.'

``I went away without understanding a word he had said.  And waited
for the one Simeon had promised.  John could not be the one; I checked
and he had been presented in the Temple more than a year before Simeon
died.

``Finally, I heard of a man preaching the Kingdom up in the north,
around the hills of Galilee.  Why would God send his messenger there?
Surely this could not be the man that was promised?

``But the more stories I heard about his preaching and teaching, the
more they seemed to align with what John had said.  There were stories
of healings, miracles, and conversions of all strata of people.  And
then I heard his name and lineage: Jesus, born of Joseph and Mary, from
the tribe of David, resident in Nazareth.  Just as it had been written
in the Temple records on the day that Simeon died.  I dropped everything
and went to hear him in the Countryside.

``I heard him preach all day, and he answered the people's questions,
and healed many.  But his answers were not like the answers of the lawyers,
even when they came in the same form.  He looked on each person with deep
compassion and answered the questions they _really_ wanted answered,
not the ones they actually asked.

``As the day wore down, and many who had to make a living or had a long
journey home drifted off, I approached him and asked him the question
that was always on my mind.

`` `Pray, Lord, what must I do to see the consolation of Israel?'

`` `What does the Law say you must do?'

``The Law?  I didn't immediately see the connection, but I quoted the
summary Rabbi Hillel had made popular in my youth: `Love the
Lord your God with all your heart, mind and soul, and love your
neigbour as yourself.'

`` `Indeed so,' said Jesus.  `Well summarized and memorized.  I
congratulate your teachers on telling you the truth.

`` `So now, _why don't you go and do it_?'

`` `But,' I protested, `these things I have done since I was a
young man just learning the law.'

`` `Have you?  Have you really loved your neighbours?'  His eyes
bore into me, and in that moment, I knew I was in the presence
of the one John had predicted.

`` `Who is my neighbour?' I asked, prompted by I know not what
force.

``He regarded me compassionately, in a way he hadn't up until that
point and launched into a story.  He told such wonderful stories;
they sounded like children's fables: but then he reached the end
of the story and suddenly you realized your life would never be
the same again.

`` `Imagine a man walking between two towns, through the open
countryside.  There are bandits in the hills, you know, who
might come down and rob him.  And, left for dead, he can hardly
help himself, can he?  So imagine a party of priests heading
through that country on their way to the Temple see him, and
recognizing he is dying, judge him unclean and are careful to
avoid him.

`` `A young man, apprenticing to be a priest, comes along, and
sees him and recognizes that he is unclean. Well taught, he
knows the commandment not to touch any unclean thing, and is
again careful to avoid him.

`` `Then a local pig-farmer comes along from Samaria.  He is
ignorant of the Temple rituals and the Law.  He sees a man
dying by the side of the road and knows that he will probably
be dead within the hour.  Notwithstanding, he takes the water
he has and gives it to the man.  He pours vinegar on his wounds
to clean them.  And he wraps him up in his cloak to keep him alive.

`` `Then he runs to the nearest inn on the road, borrows a hand cart
and a servant, returns to the body and carts it to the inn, paying
from his own purse for the innkeeper to tend him until he should
recover.

`` `You tell me who you think your neighbours are.'

``I couldn't.  In that moment, my whole life was revealed for the
fraud that it was, and I burst into tears and fell on the ground.

``By the time I could move again, he had gone.  But I was no longer
Joseph-who-waited-for-the-Kingdom but Joseph-for-whom-the-Kingdom-was-inside.

``That short encounter had healed me of my overweening pride.''

--

For a while after that, we talked of trivial incidents over the next
couple of years, and how Jesus' ministry developed.  From time to
time complaints came from rural synagogues and leaders that Jesus
was acting in ways that were disturbing `the peace'.  When possible,
he had acted within the council to demonstrate that the actions of Jesus
were on the whole acting for good, and these disturbances were just
the reaction of those who didn't like having their unholiness pointed
out.

From time to time, he had been unable to defuse the situations, and
messengers had been sent out to challenge Jesus.  Many of them came
back changed, like Joseph himself, but their encounters.  Others,
harder of heart, came back outraged by the simple fact that Jesus
cared more about sinners of all stripes than he did about important
Temple men.  Within the council, outrage started to boil.

``It all came to head when we heard that Jesus was going to Jericho.
Until that time, he had basically been in the north, and that was
largely occupied by non-believers anyway.  The preponderance of
opinion was that anything that could be done to bring the
Samaritans within the circle of Judaism was a benefit, even if we
wouldn't want that kind of heresy spreading among proper Jews.

``But suddenly, Jesus' plans seem to change.  We heard he was
heading for Jericho and then planning to come on to Jerusalem
for the Passover.

``I'm sure you know, but the big feasts are always a difficult time,
politically.  It was probably always that way, but certainly with the
Romans here, all it takes is one fool to say `Down with Rome' or
`Romans go home' and you have a riot in the streets.  Because
every proper Jew wants to celebrate the Passover in Jerusalem, the
city is always utterly crowded at that time.  And that kind of
overcrowding combined with the spirit of celebration can often lead
to unfortunate incidents.

``Well, the Sanhedrin decided that we needed to come to an
accommodation with him before he descended on Jerusalem for the
feast.  They thought that if they made a big deal out of him
being in Jericho, they could possibly deter him from coming on
to Jerusalem, at least until the feast was over.  For him to
be preaching his particular brand of radical reliance on the
God of Abraham at the Passover would probably set the spark
to the kindling.  The byplay could see Jerusalem destroyed.

``Oh, how they underestimated him!  They went to Jericho in
all their finery.  And he wandered into Jericho and didn't
even acknowledge them, but carried on with his normal routine:
tax-collectors and prostitutes.  I don't know how many souls
were saved for the coming of the Kingdom in those few days
that he was there, but quite apart from the snub that they
felt, it convinced the Sanhedrin that he could not be allowed
in the City for the Passover!

``Over the next couple of weeks, they tried everything to
stop him coming to the City.  But, as you know, none of it
worked.

``But they didn't seem to learn from what happened in Jericho.
When he came to Jerusalem, he behaved in just the same way he
had at Jericho.  The people went wild, welcoming him as the
Messiah come among them, and he just ignored the people who were
happy to see him and focused on the sinners and tax-collectors.

``And so it went all week.  They were obsessed with trying to
trap him, trick him, evict him or detain him before the Passover
came.  But nothing they tried worked.

``Then suddenly, one of his disciples came to Ananias - the
High Priest - after the Passover meal had been eaten and said that 
Jesus had celebrated it in an idolatrous fashion, mixing in
blood rituals from Greek and Roman practice, and he had been
offended and could not stand it any longer, and if we gave
him a couple of Temple guards, would arrange to capture him
quietly without bloodshed.

``Well, you know the rest.  Within a day, Jesus had been betrayed,
captured, tried, sentenced and crucified.  He had already been
sentenced by the time I heard anything about it - Ananias knew
that I would have opposed it.

``Immediately I heard, I went to Pilate and demanded an audience.
He didn't know me that well, but we had met a few times in the
course of our duties, and he respected me.

``I asked him to reverse his verdict, but he explained that he
couldn't do that for me since it wasn't really his verdict but
that of my superiors. `I did interview him,' he told me, 'but
he didn't even seem interested in escaping his death sentence.
I've never seen anybody quite as equanimious about dying.'

``I didn't know what to do.  So I did the only thing I could
think of and asked him for the body.  All that afternoon, I stood
out at Golgotha watching him die, and then as the sun was setting
they took him down and placed him on the cart my servants had brought.
We took him out to where my own tomb was, and did the minimum to
prepare the body for burial and then retreated home for the Sabbath.

``On the morning of the first day of the week, we set off early
to finish the enbalming.  And when I arrived, Mary was there.  I'd
expected to see her, but what I didn't expect was that she'd opened
the tomb!  That's a heavy stone and it had taken all of us to
close it the morning before.  I didn't see how she could have done
it herself.

``And then she told me the most fantastic story about how Jesus
was not there, but angels had spoken to her.  I went in to the tomb to look
and it was true!  The body was missing but the very linen garments
we had put on him were there.

``I didn't know what to do.  By this time Joanna was there and she
said the Peter and some of the others were at her house, and Mary went
there to tell them the news, such as it was, and I went to the Temple
to pray for guidance.

``I didn't see Jesus that evening.  I wasn't with the others when he
appeared to them.  But somehow I knew where to be on the final day, and
I was there when he led us out here to Bethany and blessed us and ascended.

``And I thought as he arose that God would come in all his majesty
and usher in the Kingdom, but that didn't happen.

``So here I am, ending my days as I began them, waiting for the coming
of the Kingdom, but having at least met the one man who could make
sense of it all.

``And like Simeon before me, I will be able to die in peace.''
