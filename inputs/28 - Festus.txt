@Chapter
title=Festus

&date AD58 Caesarea
&date 25 years after the resurrection

Paul stood in front of the new governor and the tetrach, Herod.

For a little over two years, Paul had been remanded to Philip's
house; many had seen him there, and many had been converted.  His
fame had spread, and many of the Way who had doubted his motives
had come to understand the challenges of spreading the Word amongst
the Gentiles and that Paul - in himself - was completely honest
and following the Law in every detail.  The Jewish leaders, on the other
hand, remained implacably opposed to his preaching.

Felix had been hoping that either Paul would bribe him to be
released or that the leaders of the Jews would bribe him to
sentence him to death.  In both he was disappointed.

But as with all things Roman, eventually Felix was recalled and
his replacement - Lucius Porcius Festus - arrived to take over.
A fastidious man, he could not stand the thought of cases hanging
on for two or more years and set about deciding the outstanding
backlog of cases.

In Judea, most of these were at Jerusalem, so shortly after his
arrival, he went on to Jerusalem to hear the cases that were there,
and then returned to Caesarea.

The leaders of the Jews had wished to have Paul tried in Jerusalem,
probably so that they could attempt to kill him en route as they had
planned before.  But Festus refused, arguing that he had many cases
to hear in Jerusalem but that the centre of Roman government in Judea
was at Caesarea and it made no sense to send the prisoner there.
Instead, he insisted, the Jews must make sure that there prosecutors
were in place in Caesarea when the trial commenced.

``But,'' they argued, ``this concerns matters of the Temple, which
you are not competent to judge.  We require the judgment of the High
Priest.''

``Do not tell me what I am competent to do,'' replied Festus.  ``I am
the representative of the Emperor here.  My word is as law.  Bring your
prosecutors and we will have the trial in Caesarea.  But I tell you
what I will do to calm your fears:  King Agrippa will join me on the
bench.  He understands the matters of your law, and I can rely on his
judgment.''

Obviously the leaders of the Jews were not happy at this - they could
not count of Agrippa to take their side - but they were unable to
argue with a solution that addressed the concerns that they had actually
verbalized.

So, when Festus had returned to Caesarea, he had found a day when Agrippa
and his wife Berenice were available to come to Caesarea and hear the case.
The prosecutors had arrived and were frustrated by the day until Agrippa
could arrive, especially since he would not add value to their case.

When the trial had opened, they had put forward their best arguments and called
their witnesses to condemn Paul for desecration of the Temple.  Festus
had heard them out without asking any questions.

He had then turned to Paul and asked him for his defence.
