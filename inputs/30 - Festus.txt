@Chapter
title=Festus

&date AD58 Caesarea
&date 25 years after the crucifixion

``Are you raving mad, Paul?'' interrupted Festus.  ``Have your years of
imprisonment addled your brains?  Or have you always been like this?
Should I execute you, or should I send you to a colony for the insane?''

``Rather, I would ask,'' Agrippa interjected smoothly, ``are you
trying to defend yourself against the charges brought against you,
or are you trying to convert us to being members of this Way of yours?''

``If I had to choose,'' replied Paul, ``I would choose that one man
should be saved than that I should be set free.  But, no, obviously
I am defending myself against these obviously trumped-up charges.

``The only reason that I stand here today is because I believe in
the resurrection and they do not!  It is for this that they wish
to kill me - because I am an eye-witness to the bodily Jesus,
resurrected!  And thus my defence is that while I was once like them,
now I am saved through the intercession of Jesus.  And this is what
I tell you - it is my defence.  The only possible defence against
disbelief.''

``None of this interests me,'' said Festus.  ``None of this has anything
to do with the business of the Roman Government.  I suggest that this
whole proceeding be moved back to the religious court where it originated
and the punishment that they mete out be binding.''

``But sir,'' argued the prosecutor.  ``We cannot sentence a citizen
of Tarsus to death without their consent or yours.  Yet what this man
has done is, by our laws, worthy of death.''

``That cannot be my concern,'' said Festus.  ``You may think him deserving
of death, but as Romans we abhor human sacrifice and other killings in
the name of the Gods.  Unless you have some charge that you can make
stick in this court, you can only punish him according to the limits 
of religious tolerance established by the Senate.''

``What of the riots he has caused from one end of Asia to the other?''
the prosecutor asked, temporizing.

``What concern is that of yours?'' asked Festus.  ``That would be within
the jurisdiction of courts in Asia, not here.  Do you wish me to remand him
back to Tarsus to face charges there?  If indeed, there is anyone there
that would prosecute him?''

``No,'' replied the prosecutor.  ``I wish to press the charges here.  We
have the witnesses we need.''

``I tell you, it is out of the jurisdiction of this court.  We cannot try
that here.''

``I appeal to the Senate and to Caesar!'' exclaimed Paul.  ``I am innocent
of all charges, but there is only one court that can exonerate me and that
is in Rome!

``I demand that you either release me or send me for trial at the highest
court in the Empire, presided over by Caesar!''

Festus looked at Agrippa, and then at the prosecutors before nodding
consent.  ``So noted,'' he agreed.  ``You will be transported to Rome
in the custody of Roman soldiers.  In Rome you will be held in custody
until such time as a trial can be arranged.  You will bear all the costs
of transportation and incarceration; and I want you to be aware that these
can be no small costs: I hope you have adequate funds.

``The prosecution will need to make sure that they are available for the
court on the chosen day.  Again, you will bear all the costs for your own
transportation and accomodation in Rome.  Failure to appear will obviously
prejudice your case.

``Dismissed.''
