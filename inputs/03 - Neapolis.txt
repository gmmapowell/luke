title=Ananias

&date AD 50 Aegean Sea
&date 17 years since the crucifixion

The sun came out and it was glorious.

I've met a number of men who are never happier than
when on the sea.  I am not one of them.  I view it as
a continual traitor, just waiting for you to make a
mistake before swamping you, capsizing you, stranding
you or running you aground on inhospitable rocks.

Paul said he had been shipwrecked before, but even that
couldn't kill him.  Personally, I had no desire to
test the theory.  Even without shipwreck and death, my
skills as a doctor were in continual demand at sea
with bruised and damaged fingers, broken limbs and
all manner of other minor ailments that come from being
stranded on something that doesn't stay still.

We had set off from Troas aiming for Philippi, but
as any sailor will tell you, once you set off you
don't have a great deal of control about where you
end up; the only certainty is that you will sooner
or later land _somewhere_ - unless you are unfortunate
enough to encounter a storm so violent that it sends
you to the bottom of the sea before you make landfall
anywhere.

To begin with, it had been `plain sailing' as they say,
but after three days the wind turned violent from the
south west, and pushed us back towards Asia and further
north that we wanted to go.  We battled the winds and
the seas for a whole day and night, and when we came
through the storm, we didn't really know where we were.

The wind backed up and came from the east, and we rode
it as much as we could.  The captain felt that while 
we were probably further north than we had intended,
it was best to try and make landfall as soon as possible
and then to `coast' down to Philippi.  Naturally, I agreed
enthusiastically - the sooner we were back on dry land
the better.

In the meantime, I continued to quiz Paul about his life
and adventures.

``Tell me more,'' I said, ``about how you became a follower
of the Way.  You say you used to persecute them.''

``Indeed I did,'' he replied with a wry smile.  ``And I
used to wonder at their stubbornness and pig-headedness
in the face of persecution.  But now that I know how they
felt ... well, no wonder they went to their deaths singing
hymns.''

``What happened?'' I asked.

``I Was in Damascus at the time.  I'm from Tarsus, of
course, but even then I used to travel a fair amount
both in the interests of my family business and also
to meet some of the eminent doctors of the Law and
leading Pharisees.

``There were some who felt that maybe `this Jesus' - that
was how we all used to refer to him: `this Jesus` - was
a true prophet, and that he was in line with much of
the teaching of the Pharisees.  He had an open mind and
an open heart and was obviously interested in the
welfare of the poor and lowly, particularly in the hill
country where the observation of the Law is not strict.

``It was bad enough back in the day, when the Maccabees
ruled, and the population there was largely Samaritans
and other folk who weren't truly Jews.  But then after
Pompey came and he and Caesar and that pestilential Elder Herod
starting building Graeco-Roman towns, it really went
downhill.  Anyone who could truly preach the holiness of
the Law there would be welcomed.''

``But he didn't?'' I asked.

``He did, but after his own fashion.  He did it without
respect for the form of the Law.  It wasn't enough for
him to follow the traditions that had been laid down; he
wanted to overturn _everything_; as if there were no
tomorrow.'' He paused, laughed at a private joke.  ``Of
course, that was probably _exactly_ how he felt - how
I feel now.''

``So what happened?''

``Well, you might have thought that once the leaders had
had him crucified, the whole thing would die down.  I'd
only heard rumours of it from far away, and I was in
Antioch at the time.  But then the word spread that his
followers claimed that he had been raised from the dead
and was back amongst us - well, you know what they believed;
you heard me preaching it the other night.

``The Way spread like wildfire.  Within months, I'd met
some of their followers in Antioch.  I couldn't believe
what they were preaching - even though I now preach it
myself!

``Herod the Younger tried to clamp down on it all, of
course.  But even he - and even with the support of
the Emperor - just couldn't get them to shut up.  He 
tried jail - they escaped.  He tried executing one of
their leaders, Stephen, and that just made them even
more enthusiastic.  It was becoming a real danger to
all of the Jews.  What if Claudius were to react
violently in support of his good friend Herod?''

None of this, of course, was anything I had really paid
attention to.  To be fair, at the time I was a young
slave living in Philippi and apprenticed to a doctor.
Did I care about trivial issues in far-off Judea?

But he was right.  The consequences of rebellion anywhere
within the empire were grim.  As a consequence of which,
most provincials had given up trying.  That, and the
Roman system of rewarding enterprising locals with 
the coveted citizenship among other benefits led to
a bevy of locals who actively supported Rome against
`their own' people.  Herod Agrippa, presumably, being
a case in point.

``As I say, I was in Antioch at the time, and I'd heard
that there were a number of members of this Way up in
Damascus, led by a substantial citizen of the Synagogue
called Ananias.  I'd known him for a number of years and
I was going up there to plead with him to come to his
senses and if not ... well, I was going to turn him over
to the authorities for trial.

``But I never made it.  On the contrary, he found me
when I was led into Damascus blind from a truly life-changing
experience I had while on the road there.  We were
walking along - energetically, to be sure - and hoping
to make it into the city before the gates closed at dusk,
when suddenly, I was struck with a powerful vision.  At
least, I assume it was a vision, since those who were
travelling with me were not so afflicted.

``A dazzling light appeared in the sky and a big boom
of thunder, and then there appeared to me this Jesus -
in the flesh, as it were - and he came to me and spoke.
He was calm; not angry, but intense.  The feeling was
overwhelming.  I was not afraid, but I could not hold
my ground against him.  And then, and then ... it was
like being threshed with fire.  All my beliefs, my
ignorance, my worldliness burnt off like impurities in
a smelting fire.

``He looked at me with his eyes full of a bottomless,
calm pity.  Full of love and caring, but seeing right
through me, down into the bottom of my soul.

``And the words ... the words he spoke ...

`` `Why, Saul, are you choosing to persecute me?  What
did you learn from your fathers that taught of hate and
not pity?  What did I ever do wrong that you should
try and kill me and those whose eyes I have opened?

`` `Shall I open your eyes?'

``And then he left; the vision ended.  Whether then or
before, I don't know, but I must have fallen to the
ground.  When I came to, I was curled on the ground,
hugging myself.  Those around me had heard the voice,
had heard me screaming, but seen nothing.

``And I myself could see no more.  I was blind.''

It took me a few minutes to adjust to what he was
saying.  He had met Jesus in a vision and been
rendered blind.  Was such a thing even possible?

``I don't say he made me blind,'' countered Paul.
``Merely that afterwards I was, physically blind.
But I had been spiritually blind up until that time,
and given the choice, I would rather be physically
blind that spiritually.''

``But you are not blind now,'' I commented.

``No,'' said Paul.  ``After that, I was slowly led down
the road to Damascus like a mule.  Obviously, we didn't
make it to the city gates that night; I was far too slow
for that.

``It was all so difficult.  But the physical discomfort
was nothing compared to the pain I felt in the Spirit.
It literally felt as if my skin was being flensed from
my body.  And all the time I was thinking ...

``It was like a metaphor.  The physical blindness being
compared to my spiritual blindness.  Only through the
act of losing my sight could I understand what I had
done; where I had come from.  And I saw a had a choice:
although really no choice.

``I could carry on in my ignorance and blindness; or
I could accept the will of God and move on, able to
see both physically and spiritually.  But then my whole
future would be in pawn: I could only act as God
directed me.

``For three days and nights, I was blind and fasted.  I
prayed diligently to understand the path I should follow
and the vision came to me again, of a man ... Ananias.

``He who I would have turned in to the authorities, he
it was that I saw coming to save me.  I wondered how to
reach him, given that he would know I wanted to betray
him.

``And then the miracle occurred.  He came to the house
where I was staying and greeted me as a brother.  He, too,
had had visions and had seen me wrestling with the power
of the Holy Spirit and had been charged to release me.
Only through his faith and trust was my healing possible.''

``And then?''

``Like an old man with cataracts, I was healed.  They
came away and I could see again.  Naturally, I immediately
fell to my knees and praised God before all those present.

``And then at the earliest opportunity, I left and went
into the desert.''

``Why?''

``The Lord led me.  Ever since that day he has not
ceased to be with me, to talk to me, to direct me.  And,
as I said when we met, he showed me a man, come from
Macedonia, begging for help.  You are that man.''

We continued talking for a while in this vain; I heard
about his wanderings in the desert, coming to grips with
the new teaching, and his return to Damascus, preaching
in the Synagogue the words of Jesus for the first time
- and the first riot.

Until suddenly a voice cried out from high up in the mast:
``Land! To the West! Land!''

All the passengers rushed forwards to see, but, as always,
it was another hour before the first sight of mountains
appeared over the horizon.  By then, the eagle-eyed lookout
had identified where we were.  Further north than we wanted,
to be sure, but not far from Samothrace.  Land was land, and
we could walk from there to Philippi.  It was safer.

--

``I've only been to Jerusalem a couple of times,'' Paul
confided as we walked down towards Neapolis.  ``I think
it's safe to say that basically nobody there knows me.
After leaving Damascus I went to Jerusalem and met with
the leaders of the Way there - Peter and James.  It's
strange, but they seem very inwardly focused.  If Jews
want to believe that the Messiah has come, they expound
on the preaching, but if they don't, that seems to be
fine with them.  And as for Gentiles, `first they must
become Jews and adopt the Law','' he mimicked.  ``They
don't seem to have the same pressing urgency about the
coming endtimes that I see.''

``Or maybe they just care less that everyone is saved - as
long as those who want to be saved are saved, maybe
that's enough.  After all, the rest of us are merely
Gentiles.''

He looked at me, shocked.  I don't think anybody had ever
made that exact suggestion to him before.
