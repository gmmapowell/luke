@Chapter
title=Rome

&date AD59 Rome
&date 26 years after the crucifixion

Paul's reception by the brothers of Rome was rapturous.

We had spent over three months on Malta, waiting for the seasons
to turn.

In the Spring, another Alexandrian ship had arrived in Malta to
reprovision and we were able to obtain passage from there we sailed
to Sicily, Rhegium and finally Puteoli.  We stayed there a week
before walking the rest of the way up the famed Via Appia.

Word had obviously reached Rome ahead of us that we were on our
way, and many of the brothers walked down the Appian Way and met
us at the Three Taverns Inn outside the city limits.  There Paul
greeted them and preached to them the word of the Lord as he 
received it himself.

Ever since Malta, Julius had become a firm believer in the
Word of Paul and when we reached Rome offered to accomodate
Paul and personally guarantee his safety and that he would not
be able to evade justice.

Roman justice can move quickly or slowly - as we had seen with
Felix and Festus in Judea.  When they wanted to, the magistrates
could move a trial along very quickly and make a resolution within
days.  On the other hand, when a judge was not interested in a 
case, or when he hoped for some gain by slowing it down - as Felix
had - it was possible for the proceedings to be dragged out for
months or years.

Paul - by appealing to the emperor - had only one option which
was to wait for the emperor to hear his case.  Nero had many other
distractions - including his beautiful wife, Poppaea - and hearing
a difficult and complex case from far-off Judea was, to say the
least, a long way down his priorities.

In the meantime, while Paul was held in custody at Julius' house,
he was not otherwise denied contact with his peers, and every day
saw a large number of brothers gathering in Julius' peristyle
in the middle of his house to hear Paul preach.

We had retained as a lawyer in Rome a Senator who was sympathetic
to the cause of the Way.  Lucullus had not been converted to the
Way but had known Priscilla and Aquila for many years and had
been their patron.  Lucullus came to see us frequently and to bring
us what he optimistically called `updates' on the case.

``The truth is,'' he said, ``that the emperor is not really interested
in doing any of the job of governing.  He is only really interested
in entertainment and personal diversion.  On the upside, this means
that he is generally not causing any problems in the running of the Empire
- and thus the administrators are able to do their work with little
interference from the supreme authority.  On the downside, any actions
that really do involve the personal involvement of the emperor are
often delayed for years.  And your case is one such.  I fear that,
unless the emperor comes to feel that somehow hearing your case
will achieve something for his own benefit, you could be left to
languish here for years - possibly even the rest of your life.''

``What can we do then?'' I asked, frightened.  This clear
miscarriage of justice seemed to have entrapped Paul through the
vitals and was threatening to crush out his very existence
through terminal inefficiency.

``Not very much,'' he admitted.  ``Obviously I will do everything
I can to work through my contacts to see if I can convince anybody
to speed things along.  But equally obviously, we can't push
too hard, or it will be held against us.  In the meantime, I suggest
you do the same and work with anybody you know who may have
influence to try and get a summary dismissal or else get the case
heard.  Whatever we can do to apply pressure.

``But at least in the meantime, you are free to meet people and
to continue your preaching; you may want to consider who you
preach to though - it would probably go against you if you were
to start any riots here in Rome.

``And while you are holed up here, it probably wouldn't hurt
to try and prepare your defence: why do you do what you do,
what's the history behind it, and what is your story about
the various incidents that you know are going to come up
at trial.''

``Why?'' I asked, confused.

``Roman justice is very thorough.  You have appealed to Caesar.
Caesar will not want to deal with your case.  Instead, he will
send an army of bureaucrats to look into it and then tell him
what the right thing to do is.  And bureaucrats don't want to
have to dig out all the evidence.  They want to have it given
to them on a plate - or more likely in a book bucket.''

``So give them all of our evidence in written form and make it
as exculpatory as possible?''

``And entertaining if you can manage it.  And emphasize your
loyalty to the emperor.''

And he left.

--

As he said, one of the big advantages of the Roman system of
house arrest pending trial was that the supposed-guilty party
could continue in their business provided it could be reasonably
done from their home.

Paul invited all the local Jewish elders to come and talk to him
a couple of days after his arrival.  Not all of them came of course,
but a significant number did.  Paul was not entirely unknown,
but he was not an easily recognized figure in Rome.  A number of
members of the Way had come here over the years, and some had
preached Jesus crucified and resurrected - and Paul himself had
written to the brothers in Rome while he had been in Macedonia
just before leaving on his fateful trip to Jerusalem.  He had
said in that letter that he hoped to come and see them soon - but
probably had not anticipated the sequence of events that would
lead to that coming to pass.

``I am grateful that you could make it here today,'' he began.
``I have come here at the whim of the emperor, but even so he
is directed by the one God, and it is only through his power
and authority that anything moves on this earth.  It is by
his power that we are created, and have life.  He controls
our actions and our passage through this life.

``Many of you will have heard the preaching of the Way already,
and I am sure it is a matter of some discussion and dissent
in the various synagogues of the city.  But I feel that something
has been missing in the messengers who have come before me -
a real sense of the urgency with which we are facing the future.

``The promises which came to Abraham were promises to him
for his seed.  The promise included a future revelation that
would come to his children one day and would bring about
the final judgment and rapture when men had had a chance
for repentance.

``And today that time has come!  Those are among us who have 
seen it with their own eyes, and others - myself included - who
have experienced the descent of the Holy Spirit through their own
souls.  And those of us who have encountered this Spirit know
it for what it is - the Holy One of God, his Christ.

``The prophet Isaiah prophesied that one was going to come,

`` `To bind up the broken-hearten
`` `To set at liberty them that are bruised.

``And I say unto you today that _this has happened_.  Jesus,
sent from God, born under the law and who lived without sin,
was unjustly tried and crucified, not for his sins, but that
our sins might be washed clean.

``And if that were not enough, God could not stand that Jesus
should be so crucified and acted to restore him to life - bringing
his body back from the grave as the first evidence of the
coming bodily resurrection.  This, my brothers, is what will
happen to all of us in the day of glory.  We shall all be judged
and all those who are found innocent in the Lord will be restored
in their perfect bodies _in just the same way as Jesus was restored_.

``And in this future we have all the hope of our eternal life
and our bodily resurrection.  This _is_ the promise which was
given to our father Abraham - that we should call God `Father'
and he would know us as his perfect children.

``So _now_, and I mean _right now_ is the time for all of us to
repent of our sins, and to accept the word of God for what it is
and what it means - not a dead creed of formulaic rituals and
behaviours, but a living well of guidance, as was foretold by
Jeremiah:

`` `In that day,' - meaning today - `I shall write my laws
in their inward parts, and they shall all know me from the
least of them to the greatest.'

``And this is the test by which you can measure your true
repentance and acceptance of God - when you have truly
repented and accepted God, you will receive the Holy Spirit,
and you will know the feeling of oneness with God through
his chosen intermediary - Jesus his Christ.''

It takes a lot to stop Paul in full flight, but there were
considerable murmurings running through the crowd by this point,
and one of the Elders stood up and challenged him:

``We _are_ the sons of Abraham.  What are you saying?  That
we need this Jesus to enable us to follow the Law?  I have
been studying the Law since I was a child and I can assure you
it is written in _my_ inward parts already.

``I say you are preaching a false schism to defraud the
children of Abraham from their inheritance by attempting
to deny the Law.  The Law is my guide and can be the only
guide to eternal salvation.  Deny this at your peril!''

Pandemonium broke out, as it so often had during Paul's
preaching career.  Strong opinions on both sides ruled the
day, with moderate voices in the middle - on either side
or none - ruthlessly crushed.

--

``We have a problem,'' Lucullus said the following day as
we met before dawn at Julius' house.  ``If you continue to
preach in this way, inflaming the passions of the local Jews,
you will create incidents in the public record here at Rome
that will count against you when the Emperor finally does
hear your case.  Indeed, if an incident like yesterday's were
to get out of hand, you could find yourself the recipient
of a summary judgment without so much as a hearing before the
Emperor.''
